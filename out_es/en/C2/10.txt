# UNIT: Idiomatic phrasal verbs | Expressions with dar, echar, llevar, quedar
## Lesson: Dar
dar en el blanco | hit the mark, bullseye | His argument hit the absolute mark.
dar que hablar | give people something to talk about, cause a stir | His attitude gave people a lot to talk about yesterday.
dar por sentado | take for granted | You shouldn't take anything for granted in life.
dar la nota | stand out, make a scene | His extravagant suit stood out at the gala.
dar el do supremo | give one's all, do one's utmost | He gave his absolute best in the final exam.
dar una tregua | give a break, let up | His headache isn't giving him a break today.
dar el brazo a torcer fácilmente | give in easily, yield easily | In the end, he had to give in easily.
dar la bienvenida | welcome, greet | The director welcomed the guests.
dar señales de vida | show signs of life | After the long trip, he finally showed signs of life.
dar sorpresas | give surprises | He always likes to give pleasant surprises.
## Lesson: Echar
echar pestes | badmouth, slag off | He never stops badmouthing his former boss.
echar de menos | miss | I am starting to miss my hometown.
echar la culpa | shift the blame, blame others | It's easy to always blame others.
echar a perder | ruin, spoil | A small mistake can ruin everything.
echar raíces profundas | put down deep roots | Old customs put down deep roots.
echar una ojeada | take a quick look, glance | Take a quick look at this report.
echar la bronca | tell off, reprimand | The boss told him off for arriving late.
echar rayos y trupes | fume, fly into a rage | Upon hearing the news, he was fuming with rage.
echar un vistazo | take a look | I want to take a look at that new shop.
echar cuentas | crunch numbers, do the math | We have to crunch numbers for next month.
## Lesson: Llevar
llevar a cabo | carry out, accomplish | They managed to successfully carry out the mission.
llevar una vida | lead a life | He prefers to lead a quiet and simple life.
llevar bien | get along well | I tend to get along well with all my neighbors.
llevar la contraria | be contrary, go against the grain | He always likes to contradict everyone.
llevar las riendas | take the reins, be in the driver's seat | He knows how to take the reins of the company.
llevar ventaja | have the advantage, be ahead | Our team is ahead on the scoreboard.
llevar a término | bring to a close, successfully conclude | It is difficult to bring such a complex plan to a successful conclusion.
llevar tiempo | take time | This complex project is going to take a lot of time.
llevar una temporada | be doing something for a while | He has been studying Spanish hard for a while now.
llevar la iniciativa | take the lead, take the initiative | It is important to take the initiative at work.
## Lesson: Qudar
quedar en evidencia | be shown up, be left looking foolish | His lies made him look foolish.
quedar helado | be left frozen, be dumbfounded | He left us dumbfounded with his unexpected confession.
quedar en agua de borrajas | come to nothing, fizzle out | All promises came to nothing.
quedar corto | fall short | The explanations given seemed to fall short.
quedar libre | become vacant, open up | A spot in the course will open up tomorrow.
quedar atónito | be astounded, be left astonished | I was left astonished by so much natural beauty.
quedar pendiente | remain pending, be left over | This important matter must remain pending today.
quedar satisfecho | remain satisfied, be pleased | I hope you are satisfied with the result.
quedar por debajo | fall below, stay below | Sales must not fall below zero.
quedar bien claro | be made crystal clear | The rules of the game must be made crystal clear.
## Lesson: Idioms and phrases I
pillar por sorpresa | catch by surprise, take unawares | The unexpected question caught me unawares.
quitarse un peso de encima | take a weight off one's shoulders | After submitting the work, a weight was lifted off his shoulders.
pasar página | turn the page, move on | It's time to turn the page and look to the future.
aguar la fiesta | spoil the fun, rain on one's parade | His gloomy comment came to spoil the fun.
desatar pasiones | spark passions, unleash passions | Her new novel sparked passions among critics.
sembrar la duda | sow doubt | His strange words sowed general doubt.
cortar por lo sano | make a clean break, cut it off at the root | He decided to make a clean break with that toxic partner.
tocar fondo | hit rock bottom | After the financial crisis, he managed to hit rock bottom.
llenar el ojo | catch the eye, impress | His great performance managed to impress the jury.
traer de calle | drive someone crazy, wrap around one's finger | This math problem is driving me crazy.
## Lesson: Idioms and phrases II
marcar la diferencia | make a difference | His daily effort manages to make a difference.
guardar las distancias | keep one's distance | It is wise to keep your distance from him.
levantar cabeza | get back on one's feet | Little by little he managed to get back on his feet after the blow.
poner contra las paredes | back into a corner | His question backed him into a corner.
pasar desapercibido | go unnoticed | He tried to enter the room going unnoticed.
ver la luz | see the light [gain understanding] | With the long study, he finally saw the light of knowledge.
hablar por los codos sin parar | talk nineteen to the dozen | My aunt is very cheerful and talks nineteen to the dozen.
ser un trozo de buen pan | be as good as gold, be a sweetheart | Despite his serious appearance, he is as good as gold.
mantener la compostura | keep one's composure | Despite the tension, he knew how to keep his composure.
lloriquear por todo | whine about everything | You don't like to see him whine about everything all day.
## Lesson: Expressive verbs
ganar terreno | gain ground | Clean energies continue to gain ground.
perder fuelle | lose momentum, run out of steam | The team began to lose momentum at the end.
hacer mella | take its toll, leave a mark | The hard years of work took their toll on him.
poner pegas | raise objections, throw a wrench in the works | He always likes to raise objections to every procedure.
abrir brecha | break new ground, blaze a trail | His research broke new ground in science.
perder el norte | lose one's way, lose one's bearings | With so much pressure, he ended up losing his bearings.
mantener el tipo | hold one's ground, keep a stiff upper lip | Despite the criticism, he knew how to hold his ground.
trazar una línea | draw the line | It is necessary to draw a clear line here.
tocar el cielo | touch the sky [feel ecstatic] | Upon winning the prize, he felt like he was touching the sky.
marcar el paso | set the pace | The group leader set the pace in the march.
## Lesson: Rich narration
caer en la cuenta | dawn on someone, realize | It dawned on me late how big his mistake was.
tomar el toro por los cuernos | take the bull by the horns | The director decided to take the bull by the horns.
ganarse la vida | earn a living | He works hard to earn an honest living.
llevar la voz cantante | call the shots, lead the way | In those meetings, she always calls the shots.
perder los estribos | fly off the handle, lose one's temper | A rude comment made him fly off the handle.
sacar el máximo provecho | make the most of, get the most out of | You must make the most of this experience.
hacer aguas | spring a leak, fall apart [plans] | His strategic plan began to spring a leak.
ponerse tibio | gorge oneself, feast | At the big family dinner, we gorged ourselves.
llevar la batuta principal | run the show, take the helm | In this project, he runs the show.
perder la paciencia totalmente | lose one's patience entirely | His rude attitude made me lose my patience entirely.
## Lesson: Profound verbs
llevar el agua a su molino | grind one's own axe, look out for number one | He always tries to grind his own axe.
hacer borrón y cuenta limpia | wipe the slate clean | After arguing, they decided to wipe the slate clean.
pagar los platos rotos injustamente | take the rap, bear the brunt unfairly | He always ends up taking the rap unfairly.
creerse el centro del universo | think the world revolves around oneself | His huge ego makes him think the world revolves around him.
estar con el agua hasta el cuello | be in over one's head, be drowning in debt | With so many debts, he is in over his head.
buscar una aguja imposible | look for a needle in a haystack | Finding that old paper is like looking for a needle in a haystack.
andar con mucho tiento | tread carefully, walk on eggshells | In this delicate matter, one must tread carefully.
dar gato por liebre a alguien | pull a fast one on someone, trick someone | At the market, they tried to pull a fast one on Juan.
hablar por los codos siempre | talk your ear off, chatter incessantly | My aunt is very cheerful and always chats incessantly.
ser un trozo de pan tierno | be as sweet as pie, be a heart of gold | Despite his serious appearance, he is as sweet as pie.
## Lesson: Peak verbs
llevar a buen puerto | bring to fruition, successfully conclude | His effort managed to bring the deal to fruition.
echar la casa por la ventana | spare no expense, push the boat out | He decided to spare no expense at his wedding.
quedar en tablas | end in a draw, tie | The exciting chess match ended in a draw.
dar el campanazo | cause a sensation, make a splash | His new film caused a sensation at the festival.
llevarse un chasco tremendo | be bitterly disappointed | I was bitterly disappointed when I saw the final result.
echar raíces en el corazón | take root in the heart | Her beautiful memories took root in my soul.
dar la cara por alguien | stick up for someone, stand up for someone | He is always ready to stick up for his friends.
quedar de una pieza | be flabbergasted, be struck dumb | His unexpected answer left him flabbergasted.
llevar el compás | keep the beat, keep time | With his hands, he began to keep the cheerful beat.
dar luz a una idea | shed light on an idea, inspire an idea | His profound essay shed light on a great idea.
