# UNIT: Idioms and colloquial language | Everyday idioms, slang, proverbs
## Everyday expressions 1
tirarse el pisto | show off, brag | Mateo likes to show off with his new car.
tomar el pelo | pull someone's leg, tease | Don't pull my leg with such a weird story.
sacar de quicio | drive crazy, get on one's nerves | That constant noise is driving me completely crazy.
costar un ojo de la cara | cost a arm and a leg, cost a fortune | Buying that plane ticket costs a fortune.
ahogarse en un vaso de agua | make a mountain out of a molehill | You always make a mountain out of a molehill over any little detail.
dar gato por liebre | rip off, swindle | In that cheap shop they tried to rip me off.
meter la pata | screw up, put one's foot in it | It was an important order and I screwed up with the deadline.
echar una mano | lend a hand, help out | Can you lend me a hand with this heavy box?
estar en las nubes | have one's head in the clouds, daydream | Sofia has her head in the clouds and isn't listening to the explanation.
hablar por los codos | talk a mile a minute, talk endlessly | At yesterday's dinner you talked a mile a minute with everyone.
## Everyday expressions 2
buscar tres pies al gato | look for trouble, split hairs | Don't go looking for trouble over such a simple matter.
poner los puntos sobre las íes | dot the i's and cross the t's, make things clear | The director made things clear in the meeting.
sacar los colores | make one blush, embarrass | His words of praise managed to make me blush.
perder el hilo | lose the train of thought, lose track | With so many frequent interruptions I lost the train of thought of the talk.
hablar a calzón quitado | speak frankly, mince no words | I prefer to speak frankly to resolve the conflict.
estar al tanto | be in the loop, be aware | Are you aware of the recent changes in the company?
irse por las ramas | beat around the bush, go off on tangents | Don't beat around the bush and answer the direct question.
ponerse morado | make a pig of oneself, stuff one's face | At the birthday party we stuffed our faces with cake.
dar en el clavo | hit the nail on the head | With your accurate analysis you finally hit the nail on the head.
quedarse en blanco | go blank, draw a blank | During the important oral exam my mind went completely blank.
## Slang and colloquialisms
chulo | cool, stylish | Your new leather jacket is very cool.
molar | be cool, be awesome | Young people really love going out on weekends.
guay | cool, awesome | What an awesome plan we have for this Saturday!
currar | work, graft | Lukas has to work all weekend at the office.
el curro | job, work [informal] | My new job is very close to the central station.
la pasta gansa | big bucks, a lot of money | Buying that luxury mansion requires dropping big bucks.
flipar | freak out, be blown away | Seeing the ancient monument blew my mind.
chungo | sketchy, shady, tough | The current economic situation is a bit sketchy.
mangar | swipe, pinch | Someone tried to swipe my wallet in the crowded metro.
pirarse | beat it, take off, clear out | It's very late, I think we should clear out of here now.
## Popular idioms 1
ponerse las pilas | get one's act together, pull one's socks up | You need to get your act together if you want to pass the exam.
ser pan comido | a piece of cake, a cinch | For a specialist like you, this problem is a piece of cake.
dorar la píldora | butter up, stroke someone's ego | Don't try to butter me up to achieve your goal.
bailar el agua | suck up, kiss up | There's always someone willing to suck up to the boss.
perder los papeles | lose one's cool, fly off the handle | Faced with so much unjust criticism, I lost my cool for a moment.
matar dos pájaros de un tiro | kill two birds with one stone | By shopping online we kill two birds with one stone today.
ver las estrellas | see stars [from pain] | I hit my elbow hard and saw stars.
irse de la lengua | let slip, spill the beans | I wanted to keep the secret, but I spilled the beans.
quedarse de piedra | be struck dumb, be flabbergasted | I was struck dumb upon receiving such unexpected news.
armar la marimorena | kick up a fuss, raise hell | They argued over something stupid and raised hell.
## Popular idioms 2

vivir del cuento | live off others, sponge | He intends to live off others without ever doing a stroke of work.

## Proverbs and sayings 1
a quien madruga, dios le ayuda | the early bird catches the worm | They say that the early bird catches the worm in their work.
no por mucho madrugar amanece más temprano | haste makes waste, patience is key | Have patience, remember that rushing won't make the sun rise any earlier.
cría cuervos y te sacarán los ojos | breed ravens and they will peck out your eyes | That disloyal employee proved the saying about biting the hand that feeds you.
más vale pájaro en mano que ciento volando | a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush | I prefer to accept this safe offer: a bird in the hand is better.
en boca cerrada no entran moscas | silence is golden | It's better to keep quiet sometimes; remember that silence is golden.
a palabras necias, oídos sordos | water off a duck's back, turn a deaf ear | Faced with offensive comments, always turn a deaf ear.
no hay mal que por bien no venga | every cloud has a silver lining | We missed the train, but every cloud has a silver lining.
perro ladrador, poco mordedor | barking dogs seldom bite | Don't worry about his empty threats; barking dogs seldom bite.
ojos que no ven, corazón que no siente | out of sight, out of mind | Traveling far helps forget old problems: out of sight, out of mind.
quien mucho abarca, poco apriete | biting off more than one can chew | Don't take on so many projects at once; you'll bite off more than one can chew.
## Proverbs and sayings 2
a mal tiempo, buena cara | every cloud has a silver lining, keep your chin up | Even when the crisis hits hard, remember: keep your chin up.
al pan, pan y al vino, vino | call a spade a spade, speak plainly | Things must be said clearly: let's call a spade a spade.
camino largo, andar corto | slow and steady wins the race | To achieve great goals successfully, take it slow and steady.
de tal palo, tal astilla | like father, like son, a chip off the old block | The young man plays the guitar just like his father: like father, like son.
dime con quién andas y te diré quién eres | show me your friends and I'll tell you who you are | Your new company worries me; show me your friends and I'll tell you who you are.
el hábito no hace al monkge | clothes don't make the man | Don't judge by formal appearance; remember that clothes don't make the man.
nadie sabe la gotera de su casa hasta que llueve | everyone knows where their own shoe pinches | Every family has hard secrets; everyone knows where their own shoe pinches.
no hay peor sordo que el que no quiere oir | none so deaf as those who will not hear | I tried to explain the truth to him, but none are so deaf as those who will not hear.
por la boca muere el pez | loose lips sink ships | He talked too much at the press conference and loose lips sink ships.
zapatero, a tus zapatos | stick to your knitting, mind your own business | Don't criticize my design if you don't know how; stick to your knitting.
