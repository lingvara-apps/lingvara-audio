# UNIT: Connectors and idioms | Complex sentences, connectors, everyday idioms
## Cause and reason connectors
ya que | since, seeing that | Since you have free time, come have lunch with us.
debido a que | due to the fact that, because | The flight was delayed due to the fact that there was a very strong storm.
a causa de | because of, on account of | We arrived late to the conference because of the roadworks on the highway.
gracias a que | thanks to the fact that | We passed the exam thanks to the fact that we studied together for several weeks.
por culpa de | because of [negative], due to the fault of | We missed the last train because of your unjustified delay at the station.
en vista de que | in view of the fact that, seeing as | In view of the fact that you are not replying, I will cancel the hotel reservation tomorrow itself.
a fuerza de | by dint of, through sheer | Yuki learned fluent Spanish by dint of practicing every day with natives.
por motivo de | on the grounds of, on account of | They closed the municipal library on account of urgent roof repairs.
de tanto | from so much, through too much | My throat hurt from so much singing at yesterday's concert.
pues bien | well then, now then | Well then, if you agree, we will sign the rental contract today.
## Purpose and intention connectors
con el fin de | with the aim of, in order to | We save money every month with the aim of buying a large house.
con el propósito de | with the purpose of, with the intention of | Diego traveled to Valencia with the purpose of perfecting his Spanish accent.
con miras a | with a view to, with the aim of | The company hired more staff with a view to expanding throughout Europe.
con la intención de | with the intention of, intending to | We went to the park with the intention of walking peacefully under the sun.
con el objeto de | with the object of, for the purpose of | They organized several meetings with the object of resolving neighborhood problems soon.
no sea que | lest, in case | Carry a large umbrella, lest it start raining suddenly.
no fuera a ser que | lest [past], just in case | I locked the door with a key, lest someone unknown enter.
de cara a | facing, with a view to | We are preparing new strategies facing the final exams in June.
en orden a | with a view to [formal], in order to | They adopted urgent measures in order to guarantee the safety of citizens.
para que no | so that not, in order not to | I turned off the mobile phone so that they would not disturb me during my nap.
## Contrast and concession connectors
ahora bien | however, having said that | The project looks very profitable; however, it presents certain risks that we must evaluate.
mientras que | whereas, while | Diego prefers living in the city, whereas Sofia adores the countryside.
al contrario | on the contrary, quite the opposite | I am not tired at all; on the contrary, I have a lot of energy to go out.
aun cuando | even though, even if | Omar will attend the conference even though he still has cold symptoms.
de todos modos | anyway, in any case | The ticket is quite expensive, but anyway we will go to the concert.
de todas formas | anyhow, regardless | If you cannot come today, anyhow we will meet next Friday.
así y todo | even so, nevertheless | The road was dangerous; even so, Yuki continued walking toward the summit.
bien que | although, even though [formal] | He accepted the assignment with pleasure, although the salary conditions were modest.
a pesar de ello | despite this, notwithstanding | The team played with ten players; despite this, they won the match.
mal que bien | somehow or other, more or less | We managed to finish the report on time, somehow or other, before midday.
## Result and explanation connectors
así que | so, therefore | It was pouring with rain, so we decided to stay at home reading novels.
de manera que | so that, in such a way that | She explained the lesson clearly, so that everyone understood the concepts.
conque | so then, therefore [colloquial] | So then you had no pending homework, why didn't you go out with your friends?
de suerte que | so that, luckily resulting in | He planned everything meticulously, so that no hitch arose during the trip.
por ello | for this reason, therefore | Camille forgot the keys inside the car and for this reason called the locksmith.
es más | what is more, furthermore | Ali committed no fault; what is more, he helped calm tempers.
de hecho | in fact, actually | The proposal seemed very risky; in fact, almost nobody wanted to support it at first.
en realidad | actually, really | We thought he would come early, but actually he arrived two hours late.
mejor dicho | or rather, more precisely | We are moving next month, or rather, at the beginning of July.
dicho de otra forma | in other words, put another way | The contract ended yesterday; in other words, you are completely free to decide.
## Condition and restriction connectors
a no ser que | unless, except if | Tomorrow we will go to the beach, unless it is too cold.
como no sea que | unless [colloquial], except if | We will not go to the cinema tonight, unless Diego gets tickets.
a poco que | if only a little, with just a little | If you practice every afternoon just a little, you will noticeably improve your Spanish pronunciation.
con que | provided that, as long as | Provided that you let me know half an hour in advance, I will arrive on time.
en la medida en que | to the extent that, inasmuch as | We will support your initiative to the extent that it benefits the entire community.
salvo cuando | except when, save when | I always go to work on foot, except when it rains torrentially in the morning.
a reserva de que | subject to the condition that, provided that | We will approve the annual budget, subject to the condition that they review the expense items.
excepto cuando | except when, barring when | Lukas never eats meat in restaurants, except when he travels to his village.
solo con que | just by, simply by reading | Just by reading the introduction of the book you will understand the central idea.
con solo | by just, merely by | By just hearing the first chords, Anna recognized the traditional song.
## Time and process connectors
nada más | as soon as, immediately upon | As soon as he arrived home, Diego turned on the radio to listen to music.
una vez que | once, after | Once you finish your schoolwork, you will be able to go out to play in the park.
cada vez que | every time that, whenever | Every time Omar visits our city, we meet on a central terrace.
a la vez que | at the same time as, while | Camille was listening to classical music while preparing the family dinner.
conforme | as, in line with | As the days passed, Yuki felt increasingly confident speaking.
a partir de que | from the moment that, ever since | From the moment we changed the system, sales increased remarkably.
desde que | since, ever since | Emre has not eaten meat again since he adopted that vegetarian diet.
en el instante en que | the exact moment when, just as | The exact moment when the bell rang, the students went out to the courtyard.
al poco tiempo | shortly afterwards, before long | He started studying medicine and shortly afterwards discovered his true professional vocation.
de momento | for now, at the moment | For now we have no plans for the holidays, but perhaps we will travel south.
## Organization and summary expressions
para empezar | to begin with, first of all | To begin with, I want to thank everyone for your presence at this very important event.
por una parte | on the one hand | On the one hand I like the salary, but on the other the schedule is inconvenient.
en definitiva | ultimately, all in all | Ultimately, we must choose between cutting costs or urgently seeking new international clients.
al fin y al cabo | after all | After all, we all make mistakes when learning a new foreign language.
a fin de cuentas | at the end of the day, when all is said and done | At the end of the day, the most important thing is to keep calm during the crisis.
en resumidas cuentas | in short, to sum up | In short, the economic balance of the last quarter turned out quite favorable.
dicho sea de paso | by the way, incidentally | The food was delicious and, by the way, the waiter was very attentive.
a propósito | speaking of which, by the way | Speaking of which, did you receive the email I sent you about the monthly meeting?
para colmo | to make matters worse, on top of it all | We missed the school bus and, to make matters worse, it started hailing with extreme force.
por si fuera poco | to top it all off, what is more | The new washing machine broke down and, to top it all off, we were left without electricity.
## Communication and reaction idioms
ir al grano | get straight to the point | Please, get straight to the point because we have little time before the meeting.
poner el grito en el cielo | throw a fit, raise a huge outcry | My boss threw a fit when he saw the report figures.
morderse la lengua | bite one's tongue | Camille preferred to bite her tongue so as not to worsen the discussion during lunch.
cantar las verdades | tell someone home truths, speak unvarnished truths | Ali got tired of the lies and decided to tell his colleague home truths.
hacer buenas migas | hit it off, get along well | Yuki and Sofia hit it off during their first course in Salamanca.
llevarse como el perro y el gato | fight like cat and dog | Those two brothers have been fighting like cat and dog since childhood.
cortar el bacalao | wear the pants, run the show | In that small office everyone knows who really runs the show always.
verle las orejas al lobo | see the writing on the wall, sense imminent danger | When he saw the bank debts, Maria sensed imminent danger right away.
poner el dedo en la llaga | hit the nail on the head | The journalist hit the nail on the head with his latest question about the economy.
importar un pimiento | not care a hoot, not give a damn | Diego couldn't care a hoot what they think about his new style.
## Difficulty and decision idioms
hacer de tripas corazón | put a brave face on it, steel oneself | Sofia had to steel herself and communicate the bad news to the team.
perder la cabeza | lose one's mind, go crazy | Don't lose your mind over a simple mistake that anyone can make today.
estar entre la espada y la pared | be between a rock and a hard place | Camille found herself between a rock and a hard place facing such a difficult decision.
consultar con la almohada | sleep on it | Ali preferred to sleep on it before accepting that risky job offer.
no dar un palo al agua | not lift a finger | His younger brother spends the entire summer without lifting a finger.
tener agallas | have the guts, have courage | Lukas had the guts to report the irregularities committed in his former company.
quedarse de brazos cruzados | sit back and do nothing, stand idly by | Emre did not want to stand idly by while his neighbors needed urgent help.
cruzar los dedos | cross one's fingers | We will cross one's fingers so that Maria gets the full medical research scholarship.
costar un riñón | cost an arm and a leg | The plane ticket to Tokyo cost him an arm and a leg during Christmas.
pasar la noche en blanco | pull an all-nighter | Diego pulled an all-nighter reviewing the final details of his thesis.
## State and result idioms
hacer castillos en el aire | build castles in the air, daydream | Sofia prefers working hard every day instead of building castles in the air.
pedir peras al olmo | ask for the impossible | Demanding punctuality from Omar is like asking for the impossible almost always.
ver los toros desde la barrera | sit on the sidelines, watch safely from afar | It is very comfortable to sit on the sidelines and criticize others.
arrimar el ascua a su sardina | grind one's own axe, feather one's own nest | In that heated discussion, Ali tried to feather his own nest discreetly.
ser uña y carne | be thick as thieves, inseparable | Yuki and Camille have been thick as thieves since they met at university.
llevarse la palma | take the cake, carry off the prize | Among all the desserts in the contest, Lukas's homemade tart carried off the prize.
tomar el pulso | feel the pulse, test the waters | Before announcing the reforms, the mayor wanted to test the waters with public opinion.
cantar victoria | count one's chickens before they hatch, claim victory too early | You shouldn't count your chickens before they hatch yet because several decisive minutes remain in the match.
dar una cabezada | take a nap, catnap | Anna took advantage of the long train journey to take a restorative catnap.
estar al loro | keep one's eyes peeled, stay alert | Diego recommended his friends keep their eyes peeled for cheap flight offers.
