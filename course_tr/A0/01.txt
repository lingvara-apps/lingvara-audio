# UNIT: The Turkish alphabet | 29 letters, ç ğ ı ö ş ü, the dotted and dotless i, the circumflex
## Letters A – H
A a | like the "a" in "father", but short; letter name "a" | harf · sesli | araba | car
B b | like the "b" in "boy"; letter name "be" | harf · ünsüz | balık | fish
C c | always like the "j" in "jam", never like "k" or "s"; letter name "ce" (jeh) | harf · ünsüz | cep | pocket
Ç ç | like the "ch" in "church"; letter name "çe" (cheh) | harf · ünsüz | çay | tea
D d | like the "d" in "day"; letter name "de" | harf · ünsüz | deniz | sea
E e | like the "e" in "bed"; letter name "e" | harf · sesli | ev | house
F f | like the "f" in "fun"; letter name "fe" | harf · ünsüz | fincan | cup
G g | always hard like the "g" in "go", never like "gem"; letter name "ge" | harf · ünsüz | gece | night
Ğ ğ | "soft g": never starts a word and is not really pronounced; it lengthens the vowel before it; letter name "yumuşak ge" | harf · ünsüz | dağ | mountain
H h | like the "h" in "hat", always pronounced, even at the end of a word; letter name "he" | harf · ünsüz | hayır | no
## Letters I – P
I ı | dotless i: like the "a" in "about" or the "e" in "roses", lips relaxed and unrounded; letter name "ı" | harf · sesli | ılık | lukewarm
İ i | dotted i: like the "ee" in "see", but short; the capital keeps its dot: İ | harf · sesli | ip | rope
J j | like the "s" in "measure"; rare, mostly in words from French | harf · ünsüz | jilet | razor blade
K k | like the "k" in "kite"; letter name "ke" | harf · ünsüz | kapı | door
L l | light like the "l" in "leaf" next to e, i, ö, ü; darker like in "ball" next to a, ı, o, u; letter name "le" | harf · ünsüz | limon | lemon
M m | like the "m" in "man"; letter name "me" | harf · ünsüz | masa | table
N n | like the "n" in "no"; letter name "ne" | harf · ünsüz | nar | pomegranate
O o | like the "o" in "more", short and pure, never "ow"; letter name "o" | harf · sesli | okul | school
Ö ö | say the "e" in "bed" while rounding your lips (like German ö); letter name "ö" | harf · sesli | ördek | duck
P p | like the "p" in "pen"; letter name "pe" | harf · ünsüz | para | money
## Letters R – Z and the circumflex
R r | a quick tap of the tongue, as in Spanish "pero"; at the end of a word it sounds softer, almost whispered; letter name "re" | harf · ünsüz | resim | picture
S s | always like the "s" in "sun", never like "z"; letter name "se" | harf · ünsüz | su | water
Ş ş | like the "sh" in "ship"; letter name "şe" (sheh) | harf · ünsüz | şeker | sugar
T t | like the "t" in "top"; letter name "te" | harf · ünsüz | tuz | salt
U u | like the "oo" in "food", but short; letter name "u" | harf · sesli | uçak | plane
Ü ü | say the "ee" in "see" while rounding your lips (like German ü or French u); letter name "ü" | harf · sesli | üzüm | grapes
V v | like the "v" in "very", but softer, with the lips barely touching; letter name "ve" | harf · ünsüz | vapur | ferry
Y y | always a consonant, like the "y" in "yes"; letter name "ye" | harf · ünsüz | yol | road
Z z | like the "z" in "zoo"; letter name "ze" | harf · ünsüz | zeytin | olive
Â â | circumflex (şapka): makes a vowel long or softens the k, g or l before it; not a separate letter; Turkish has no q, w or x | harf · sesli | kâğıt | paper
# UNIT: Special sounds and vowel harmony | Front and back vowels, harmony in suffixes, soft ğ, tricky consonants, stress
## Front and back vowels
a, ı, o, u | back vowels: made at the back of the mouth; they sound "deep" | ses | kutu | box
e, i, ö, ü | front vowels: made at the front of the mouth; they sound "bright" | ses | gömlek | shirt
ı | relax your tongue and lips and say the "a" of "sofa"; keep it short | ses | kırk | forty
i / ı | the dot changes the word: "kil" (clay) and "kıl" (hair) are different words | ses | ışık | light
ö | round your lips as for "o", then say "e" without moving them | ses | köprü | bridge
ü | round your lips as for "u", then say "i" without moving them | ses | gül | rose
o / ö | "on" means ten, "ön" means front: listen for the brighter ö | ses | ön | front
u / ü | "uç" means tip, "üç" means three: listen for the brighter ü | ses | dün | yesterday
aa, ee | a double vowel is said long, often with a tiny break: "sa-at" | ses | saat | hour; clock
pure vowels | Turkish vowels never glide: "ben" is not "bain", "yol" is not "yowl" | ses | ders | lesson
## Vowel harmony
-lar / -ler | plural: after a, ı, o, u add -lar; after e, i, ö, ü add -ler | ses | kitaplar | books
-ı / -i / -u / -ü | four-way harmony: a, ı → ı; e, i → i; o, u → u; ö, ü → ü | ses | evi | his house
-da / -de | "in, at, on": the vowel follows the last vowel of the word | ses | evde | at home
-dan / -den | "from": the same two-way harmony | ses | okuldan | from school
-ta / -te | after the hard consonants ç f h k p s ş t, the d of a suffix becomes t | ses | parkta | in the park
mı / mi / mu / mü | the question word is written separately but still follows harmony | ses | güzel mi? | is it nice?
-lı / -li / -lu / -lü | "with": four-way harmony | ses | şekerli | with sugar
-sız / -siz / -suz / -süz | "without": four-way harmony | ses | sütsüz | without milk
saat → saatler | some borrowed words break harmony and take front-vowel suffixes | ses | saatler | hours; clocks
-yor | the present tense ending never changes; only the vowel before it harmonises | ses | geliyor | he is coming
## Soft ğ and tricky consonants
ağ, ığ, oğ, uğ | after a back vowel, ğ just makes the vowel longer: "ağaç" sounds like "aaaç" | ses | ağaç | tree
eğ, iğ, öğ, üğ | after a front vowel, ğ sounds like a light "y": "değil" sounds like "deyil" | ses | değil | not
-ğ (end) | at the end of a word, ğ lengthens the last vowel: "yağ" sounds like "yaa" | ses | yağ | oil; fat
c / ç | "cam" (glass) has a "j" sound, "çam" (pine) has a "ch" sound | ses | çam | pine
s / ş | "su" (water) and "şu" (that) are different words | ses | şişe | bottle
j / c | j is soft like "measure", c is hard like "jam" | ses | garaj | garage
r (end) | a final r is soft and slightly breathy, almost like "rh" | ses | kar | snow
h (middle, end) | h is always heard, even in the middle or at the end of a word | ses | sabah | morning
v (after o, u) | between vowels and after o or u, v sounds lighter, close to English "w" | ses | tavuk | chicken
kâ, gâ, lâ | a circumflex after k, g or l adds a light "y": "kâr" sounds like "kyar" | ses | dükkân | shop
## Stress and spelling rules
final stress | most Turkish words are stressed on the last syllable: ka-LEM | ses | kalem | pen
moving stress | when you add a suffix, the stress moves to the new last syllable: a-ra-ba-LAR | ses | arabalar | cars
place-name stress | most place names are stressed earlier: AN-ka-ra, i-STAN-bul | ses | Ankara | Ankara
negative -ma / -me | the syllable before -ma or -me is stressed: GEL-me-dim | ses | gelmedim | I didn't come
adverb stress | many adverbs and question words stress the first syllable: ŞİM-di, NE-re-de | ses | şimdi | now
double consonants | double letters are held a little longer: an-ne | ses | anne | mother
p ç t k → b c d ğ | before a vowel, a final p, ç, t or k often softens: kitap → kitabı | ses | kitabı | his book
buffer y, n, s | when two vowels meet, a y (or n, s) is inserted: araba + ı → arabayı | ses | arabayı | the car (as object)
apostrophe | suffixes on names are separated with an apostrophe: Ankara'da | ses | İzmir'de | in Izmir
İ / I capitals | i becomes İ and ı becomes I in capitals: İstanbul, Irmak | ses | İstanbul | Istanbul
