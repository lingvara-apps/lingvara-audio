# UNIT: Rhétorique et débat | Débat, art de persuader, analyse des médias
## Les bases du débat
motion | motion, sujet de débat | The motion for tonight's debate concerns banning cars from the city centre.
rebuttal | réfutation, contre-argument | Her rebuttal exposed several weaknesses in the other team's case.
proponent | partisan, défenseur | Proponents of the plan say it will reduce traffic considerably.
moderator | modérateur, animateur | The moderator reminded both speakers to respect the time limit.
concede | concéder, admettre | Diego conceded that his opponent had made a fair point.
counterargument | contre-argument | A strong essay should address at least one counterargument.
premise | prémisse | The whole argument rests on a false premise.
stance | position, point de vue | What is your stance on working from home?
contend | soutenir, affirmer | Critics contend that the new law will hurt small businesses.
refute | réfuter | Camille refuted every claim with clear evidence and calm logic.
## Construire un argument
assertion | assertion, affirmation | His assertion was not supported by any reliable data.
substantiate | étayer, prouver | Can you substantiate that claim with real figures?
validity | validité | Several experts questioned the validity of the survey results.
coherent | cohérent | Sofia presented a coherent argument from start to finish.
compelling | convaincant, captivant | The lawyer made a compelling case for her client's innocence.
flawed | erroné, faussé | The study was flawed because the sample was too small.
underpin | étayer, sous-tendre | Solid research underpins every claim in her speech.
contention | thèse, affirmation | It is my contention that schools should start later in the morning.
deduction | déduction | His deduction seemed logical, but it ignored one important fact.
plausible | plausible | Her explanation sounds plausible, but we still need proof.
## Le langage persuasif
appeal | séduire, faire appel (à) | The advert appeals to parents who worry about their children's health.
emotive | émouvant, à charge émotionnelle | Politicians often use emotive language to win support.
sway | influencer, faire fléchir | Nothing he said could sway the undecided voters.
rhetorical | rhétorique | That was a rhetorical question, so you don't need to answer it.
hyperbole | hyperbole, exagération | Saying the queue was a mile long was pure hyperbole.
underscore | souligner, accentuer | The speaker underscored the need for urgent action on pollution.
credibility | crédibilité | One factual error can damage a speaker's credibility.
win over | rallier, convaincre | Her honest answers won over even the sceptical audience.
manipulate | manipuler | Some advertisers manipulate our fears to sell their products.
catchphrase | slogan, phrase choc | The campaign's catchphrase was repeated on every billboard in town.
## Prise de parole et présentation
lectern | pupitre | Sofia placed her notes on the lectern and smiled at the audience.
rapport | relation de confiance, contact | A good speaker builds rapport with the audience in the first minute.
anecdote | anecdote | He opened his talk with a funny anecdote about his first job.
podium | estrade, podium | The mayor stepped up to the podium and thanked everyone.
digress | digresser | Sorry, I digress; let's return to the main question.
concise | concis | Keep your answers concise because each speaker has two minutes.
recap | récapituler | Let me briefly recap the three main points of today's talk.
ramble | divaguer, s'éterniser | When he gets nervous, he tends to ramble for ages.
captivate | captiver | Her story captivated the audience from the very first minute.
heckle | chahuter, interpeller | A man at the back began to heckle the speaker.
## Analyse des médias
slant | orientation (politique), biais | The article had a clear slant against the new mayor.
coverage | couverture médiatique | The coverage of the floods was detailed and fair.
sensationalism | sensationnalisme | Readers are tired of sensationalism and want calm, accurate reporting.
misleading | trompeur, mensonger | The headline was misleading and caused unnecessary panic.
editorial | éditorial | Today's editorial criticises the government's housing policy.
spin | orientation habile, manipulation d'image | The minister's team put a positive spin on the bad figures.
scrutiny | examen minutieux, contrôle | The company's accounts are now under close scrutiny.
fact-check | vérifier les faits | Journalists should fact-check every quote before publishing it.
objectivity | objectivité | A good reporter tries to maintain objectivity at all times.
sound bite | petite phrase, extrait sonore | The evening news showed only a short sound bite from the interview.
## Accord et désaccord
concur | être d'accord, concorder | I concur with my colleague on this particular point.
dissent | dissension, opposition | There was strong dissent among the members about the new rules.
misgiving | appréhension, doute | Many residents expressed serious misgivings about the new parking plan.
contradict | contredire | His latest statement contradicts what he said last week.
dispute | contester, remettre en cause | Nobody seriously disputes that the climate is changing.
acknowledge | reconnaître | She acknowledged that the plan had some weaknesses.
side with | prendre parti pour | In the end, most of the class sided with Omar on this issue.
dismiss | rejeter, balayer (d'un revers de main) | He dismissed the criticism as unfair and badly informed.
take issue with | contester, émettre des réserves | I take issue with your claim that young people never read.
common ground | terrain d'entente | Despite their differences, the two sides found some common ground.
## Preuves et logique
anecdotal | anecdotique, non scientifique | The evidence is only anecdotal and needs proper research.
generalization | généralisation | Saying all teenagers are lazy is an unfair generalization.
assumption | supposition, hypothèse | Your plan is based on the assumption that prices will fall.
conclusive | probant, concluant | There is no conclusive proof that the new drug works.
irrelevant | hors sujet, non pertinent | Her age is irrelevant to the question we are discussing.
inconsistency | incohérence | The reporter noticed an inconsistency in the witness's story.
circular | circulaire (raisonnement) | His reasoning is circular because the conclusion simply repeats the premise.
verify | vérifier | We could not verify the information from any other source.
rationale | justification, logique | Can you explain the rationale behind this decision?
correlation | corrélation | Correlation does not always mean that one thing causes another.
## Le ton du débat
heated | houleux, animé | The meeting turned into a heated argument about money.
civil | courtois, poli | Let's keep this discussion civil and avoid personal comments, please.
confrontational | conflictuel | His confrontational style made the negotiations even harder.
cut in | couper la parole, intervenir | Sorry to cut in, but I have a quick question.
backtrack | faire marche arrière | The company was forced to backtrack after customers complained loudly.
bicker | se disputer pour des broutilles | The two brothers bicker about everything, even the weather.
hostile | hostile | The audience grew hostile when the speaker ignored their questions.
composure | sang-froid, maîtrise de soi | Even under pressure, Emre never lost his composure.
talk over | couper la parole, parler par-dessus | It is rude to talk over people who disagree with you.
patronising | condescendant | Please don't use that patronising tone when you explain it to me.
## Opinion publique et campagnes
petition | pétition | Over ten thousand people signed the petition to save the park.
advocate | prôner, plaider pour | Maria advocates more cycle lanes in the city.
lobby | faire du lobbying | Local farmers lobbied the council for lower water prices.
grassroots | de la base, populaire | The grassroots movement started with just a few neighbours.
rally | rassemblement, meeting | Thousands of people attended the rally in the main square.
mobilize | mobiliser | The group mobilized hundreds of volunteers through social media.
controversy | polémique, controverse | The new stadium caused controversy among local residents.
propaganda | propagande | The posters were pure propaganda with no real facts.
outcry | tollé, lever de boucliers | The plan to close the hospital caused a public outcry.
backlash | retour de bâton, réaction hostile | The decision to raise ticket prices led to a strong backlash from fans.
## Expressions de débat
all things considered | tout bien réfléchi | All things considered, the debate went better than we expected.
by the same token | de même, par la même occasion | Teachers should respect students; by the same token, students should respect teachers.
it stands to reason | il va de soi, c'est évident | It stands to reason that tired drivers cause more accidents.
play devil's advocate | jouer l'avocat du diable | Let me play devil's advocate and argue for the opposite view.
beg the question | soulever une question évidente | The high price begs the question of who can actually afford it.
moot point | question discutable, point sans objet | Whether we could have won is now a moot point.
in a nutshell | en un mot, en résumé | In a nutshell, the project failed because of poor planning.
the long and the short of it | en un mot, bref | The long and the short of it is that we need more time.
for the sake of argument | pour les besoins de la cause | For the sake of argument, let's say the price doubles next year.
conversely | inversement, en revanche | Some people find the city exciting; conversely, others find it exhausting.
