# UNIT: Nuances de caractère | Traits de personnalité fins et comportements
## Qualités positives
conscientious | consciencieux, travailleur | She is a conscientious worker who never misses a deadline.
resourceful | débrouillard, ingénieux | Omar is resourceful and always finds a way to fix things.
compassionate | compatissant, empathique | The nurse was compassionate towards every frightened patient on the ward.
magnanimous | magnanime, généreux | He was magnanimous in victory and praised his opponent warmly.
diligent | diligent, assidu | Yuki is a diligent student who reviews her notes every evening.
level-headed | pondéré, pragmatique | We need a level-headed person to handle this crisis calmly.
open-minded | ouvert d'esprit | My grandmother is surprisingly open-minded about new ideas and lifestyles.
tactful | plein de tact, diplomate | Try to be tactful when you tell him about the mistake.
unassuming | sans prétention, modeste | Despite his success, Lukas remained a quiet and unassuming man.
dependable | fiable, digne de confiance | Anna is the most dependable colleague I have ever worked with.
## Défauts
conceited | vaniteux, prétentieux | He is so conceited that he never admits he is wrong.
obstinate | obstiné, têtu | The obstinate child refused to put on his coat in the rain.
spiteful | malveillant, vindicatif | That was a spiteful comment, and I think you know it.
gullible | naïf, crdule | Don't be so gullible; that email is obviously a scam.
self-centred | égoïste, centré sur soi | A self-centred person rarely asks how other people are feeling.
petty | mesquin, pinaud | Arguing about who paid for the coffee seems rather petty.
vain | vain, narcissique | He is too vain to wear his glasses in public.
moody | d'humeur changeante, lunatique | Teenagers can be moody, especially early in the morning.
manipulative | manipulateur | She finally realised that her friend had been manipulative for years.
tight-fisted | radin, avare | My tight-fisted uncle never leaves a tip in restaurants.
## Comportements sociaux
gregarious | grégaire, sociable | Diego is gregarious and loves big, noisy family dinners.
reserved | réservé, distant | At first Emre seemed reserved, but he is actually very warm.
aloof | distant, détaché | The new manager seemed aloof and rarely chatted with the staff.
approachable | abordable, accessible | Our professor is approachable, so students ask her lots of questions.
considerate | prévenant, attentionné | It was very considerate of you to call before visiting us.
nosy | curieux, fouineur | Our nosy neighbour always asks where we are going and why.
boisterous | exubérant, bruyant | The boisterous fans sang loudly all the way home.
courteous | courtois, poli | The hotel staff were courteous and helpful throughout our stay.
tactless | maladroit, manquant de tact | His tactless joke about her age upset everyone at the table.
introvert | introverti | As an introvert, Sofia recharges by spending time alone with a book.
## Style de travail
meticulous | méticuleux, rigoureux | Camille is meticulous about checking every figure in the report twice.
methodical | méthodique, ordonné | A methodical approach will save us a lot of time in the long run.
proactive | proactif, prévoyant | Be proactive and solve problems before they get any worse.
sloppy | bâclé, négligé | The report was sloppy and full of spelling mistakes.
perfectionist | perfectionniste | As a perfectionist, Maria finds it hard to finish her projects.
persistent | persistant, persévérant | If you are persistent, you will eventually get an interview somewhere.
single-minded | déterminé, focalisé | She was single-minded in her pursuit of a career in medicine.
laid-back | détendu, relax | Our laid-back boss doesn't mind if we arrive a little late.
workaholic | bourreau de travail, accro au boulot | Ali became a workaholic and completely forgot to take holidays.
complacent | complaisant, suffisant | After two easy wins, the team became complacent and lost.
## Réactions émotionnelles
thin-skinned | susceptible, à fleur de peau | Don't be so thin-skinned; it was only a friendly joke.
hot-headed | impulsif, chaud bouillant | The hot-headed player was sent off after only ten minutes.
even-tempered | calme, égal d'humeur | My father is even-tempered and rarely raises his voice at anyone.
irritable | irritable, colérique | I get irritable when I haven't had enough sleep.
resilient | résistant, résilient | Children are often far more resilient than adults think.
temperamental | caractériel, lunatique | The temperamental chef shouted whenever a dish was late.
sentimental | sentimental | She is very sentimental and keeps every birthday card she receives.
excitable | excitable, nerveux | Our excitable puppy jumps on every visitor at the door.
melancholic | mélancolique, sombre | The film had a melancholic ending that stayed with me for days.
composed | maître de soi, posé | Even under pressure, the pilot remained calm and composed.
## Style de communication
outspoken | franc, direct | She is outspoken and always says exactly what she thinks.
blunt | abrupt, sans ménagement | His blunt feedback hurt a little, but it was really useful.
articulate | articulé, éloquent | Omar is an articulate speaker who explains complex ideas clearly.
chatty | bavard | The chatty taxi driver told us his whole life story.
sarcastic | sarcastique, caustique | He made a sarcastic remark about my cooking in front of guests.
witty | spirituel, plein d'esprit | Her witty speech made the whole room burst out laughing.
soft-spoken | à la voix douce, calme | The soft-spoken teacher never needed to shout at her pupils.
evasive | évasif | When I asked about the money, he suddenly became evasive.
condescending | condescendant, hautain | I hate it when people use a condescending tone with waiters.
eloquent | éloquent | The bride's father gave an eloquent and moving speech at the wedding.
## Attitude et point de vue
cynical | cynique | After many years in politics, he had become rather cynical.
naive | naïf | It was naive of me to believe all his promises.
pragmatic | pragmatique | We need a pragmatic solution now, not a perfect one.
idealistic | idéaliste | As a young teacher, Lukas was idealistic and full of energy.
narrow-minded | étroit d'esprit | It is narrow-minded to judge people by their accent.
indecisive | indécis, irrésolu | I'm so indecisive that choosing a restaurant takes me ages.
judgmental | moralisateur, critique | Try not to be judgmental when your friends share their problems.
self-assured | sûr de soi | She gave a calm and self-assured presentation to the board.
apathetic | apathique, indifférent | Many young people feel apathetic about local elections.
principled | intègre, de principe | A principled journalist refuses to publish rumours without checking them.
## Verbes d'action
boast | se vanter, fanfaronner | He likes to boast about his new car to everyone.
sulk | faire la têtes, bouder | Stop sulking and tell me what is wrong.
flatter | flatter | You are only flattering me because you want a favour.
nag | harceler, faire des reproches | My mother keeps nagging me about tidying my room.
procrastinate | procrastiner, remettre au lendemain | I always procrastinate when I have a long essay to write.
show off | faire le malin, frimer | He only bought that expensive watch to show off.
meddle | se mêler de, s'immiscer | Please don't meddle in our family affairs any more.
grumble | grommeler, rouspéter | Diego grumbled about the weather all weekend long.
dither | hésiter, tergiverser | Stop dithering and just choose a colour for the walls.
tease | taquiner | Her brothers used to tease her about her curly hair.
## Noms pour décrire les gens
extrovert | extraverti | Emre is a true extrovert who loves meeting new people.
know-it-all | je-sais-tout, monsieur-je-sais-tout | Nobody likes a know-it-all who corrects everyone in meetings.
busybody | fouineur, fouteur de merde | The village busybody seemed to know everyone's secrets.
pushover | bonasse, personne influençable | Don't be a pushover; say no if you're too busy.
daydreamer | rêveur, luneux | The daydreamer in the back row never heard the teacher's question.
go-getter | fonceur, ambitieux | The company is looking for a young and energetic go-getter.
couch potato | pantouflard, patate de canapé | My brother is a couch potato who watches TV all weekend.
troublemaker | fauteur de troubles, perturbateur | The teacher moved the troublemaker to the front of the class.
role model | modèle, exemple | Her grandmother has always been her greatest role model.
loner | solitaire | He was a loner at school but made many friends at university.
## Expressions idiomatiques de caractère
a heart of gold | avoir un cœur d'or | Behind his rough voice, Ali really has a heart of gold.
a cold fish | une crème glacée, une personne froide | Her new boyfriend seems a bit of a cold fish at first.
down-to-earth | terre à terre, les pieds sur terre | Despite her success, Maria is very friendly and down-to-earth.
a wet blanket | un rabat-joie | Don't be such a wet blanket; come dancing with us!
full of oneself | imbu de sa personne | Since his promotion, he has been completely full of himself.
thick-skinned | insensible aux critiques, blindé | You need to be thick-skinned to work as a stand-up comedian.
a people person | très sociable, aime les gens | As a real people person, she is perfect for customer service.
keep oneself to oneself | rester discret, vivre dans son coin | Our neighbour is quiet and mostly keeps himself to himself.
a social butterfly | papillon social, mondain | At parties Sofia is a real social butterfly who talks to everyone.
two-faced | hypocrite, double face | I can't trust him any more because he is so two-faced.
