# UNIT: Langage familier | Particules, interjections, argot courant
## Les mots de remplissage
you know | tu vois, n'est-ce pas | It was one of those days when, you know, nothing really works.
I mean | je veux dire | The film was fine, I mean, it just wasn't what I expected.
kind of | un peu, en quelque sorte | I'm kind of tired after the trip, so let's stay in tonight.
sort of | genre, pour ainsi dire | Emre sort of agreed, but he didn't sound very convinced.
basically | en gros, fondamentalement | Basically, we need more time and a bit more money to finish.
literally | littéralement, vraiment | I was literally the only person in the whole cinema last night.
or something | ou un truc comme ça | Let's grab a coffee or something before the meeting starts.
and stuff | et tout le tralala | We talked about work and stuff, nothing too serious really.
if you like | si l'on peut dire | The village was, if you like, a little world of its own.
you see | tu vois, écoute | You see, I never learned to drive, so I always take the bus.
## Les interjections
whoa | ouh là, attends | Whoa, slow down, you're driving far too fast on this road!
gosh | mon dieu, mince | Gosh, I didn't realise it was already so late in the evening.
blimey | purée, mazette (britannique) | Blimey, have you seen how much they charge for parking here?
yikes | ouille, aïe | Yikes, I just noticed I sent that email to the wrong person.
whoops | oups | Whoops, I think I've just spilled coffee all over your notes.
phew | ouf, heureusement | Phew, we caught the last train just before the doors closed.
ugh | beurk, pfff | Ugh, it's raining again and I forgot my umbrella at home.
eww | pouah, beurk | Eww, there's something sticky all over the handle of this door.
aha | ah bon, tiens | Aha, so that's why the printer kept jamming all morning!
huh | hein | Huh, I never thought Omar would actually move abroad one day.
## Les expressions de réaction
no way | sans blague, c'est pas vrai | No way, you got the job after only one interview?
you bet | tu m'étonnes, carrément | Are you coming to the party on Saturday? You bet I am!
fair enough | d'accord, c'est pas faux | You need more time to decide? Fair enough, let's talk next week.
tell me about it | ne m'en parle pas | This traffic is unbearable today. Tell me about it, I've been stuck for an hour.
you don't say | sans blague, tu m'en diras tant | The shop is closed on Sunday? You don't say, what a surprise.
big deal | et alors, c'est pas la mer à boire | So he has a new car, big deal, it doesn't make him a better person.
so what | et alors | So what if we're a little late, the party goes on until midnight.
my bad | c'est ma faute, autant pour moi | My bad, I totally forgot to send you the address yesterday.
no kidding | sans blague, tu rigoles | No kidding, Yuki really ran the whole marathon without any training.
go figure | cherche à comprendre | He hates travelling but became a pilot, go figure.
## Salutations et apostrophes
mate | mon pote, l'ami (britannique) | Cheers for the help yesterday, mate, I really owe you one.
buddy | vieux, mon pote (américain) | Hey buddy, could you give me a hand with these heavy boxes?
dude | mec, mon vieux (américain) | Dude, you have to try the burgers at that new place downtown.
folks | les gens, tout le monde | Okay folks, the bus leaves in ten minutes, so please don't be late.
cheers | merci, santé | Cheers for holding the door, that bag was heavier than I thought.
ta | merci (familier britannique) | Ta for the tea, love, it's exactly what I needed after work.
howdy | salut (régional américain) | Howdy, neighbour, are you coming to the barbecue this weekend?
what's up | quoi de neuf, salut | What's up, Diego? I haven't heard from you in weeks.
long time no see | ça fait un bail | Long time no see, Camille, you look great after your holiday!
catch you later | à plus tard | I've got to run to my lecture now, catch you later at lunch.
## Adjectifs argotiques
gutted | dégoûté, abattu | Lukas was absolutely gutted when his team lost the final.
knackered | crevé, lessivé | I'm completely knackered after moving furniture all day long.
chuffed | ravi, enchanté | Maria was really chuffed with the birthday present we got her.
dodgy | louche, suspect | That website looks a bit dodgy, I wouldn't enter my card details there.
skint | fauché | I'd love to come to the festival, but I'm a bit skint this month.
hyped | survolté, surexcité | The kids are so hyped about the school trip to the seaside.
chill | détendu, cool | The new manager is pretty chill, she never shouts at anyone.
peckish | un brin faim, creux | I'm feeling a bit peckish, shall we grab a sandwich somewhere?
gobsmacked | sidéré, baba | We were gobsmacked when Ali announced he was leaving for Australia.
minted | blindé, riche | Her parents must be minted to own a house like that by the sea.
## Noms argotiques
bloke | mec, type (britannique) | The bloke next door helped me fix my bike this morning.
quid | livres sterling, balles (argot) | The tickets were only ten quid each, so we bought four.
buck | dollars, balles (argot) | Can you lend me twenty bucks until Friday?
grand | mille (argot) | They paid about five grand for that old car, which seems a lot.
telly | la téloche, la télé (britannique) | There's nothing good on the telly tonight, let's read instead.
loo | les toilettes, chiottes (britannique) | Excuse me, do you know where the loo is in this building?
fiver | un billet de cinq (argot) | I found a fiver in the pocket of my old winter coat.
grub | la bouffe | The grub at the student canteen is cheap but surprisingly tasty.
cuppa | une tasse de thé (britannique) | Sit down and have a cuppa, you look like you need a break.
brekkie | le p'tit déj (argot) | We had a huge brekkie at the café before going for a hike.
## Verbes argotiques
crash | squatter, pioncer | Can I crash at your place tonight? I missed the last bus.
bail | lâcheur, filer à l'anglaise | Sorry, I have to bail tonight, something came up at work.
ditch | lâcher, semer | We ditched the car and walked the rest of the way into town.
freak out | flipper, paniquer | Don't freak out, but I think I've lost the keys to the flat.
binge | s'enfiler d'affilée, bingewatcher | We binged the whole series in one weekend and regretted nothing.
splurge | flamber, craquer son pognon | After the exams, Anna splurged on a fancy dinner with her friends.
nick | piquer, faucher (britannique) | Someone nicked my bike from outside the library yesterday afternoon.
chuck | balancer, jeter | Just chuck your coat on the sofa and make yourself at home.
fancy | avoir envie de, kiffer | Do you fancy going to the cinema after dinner tonight?
reckon | estimer, penser | I reckon it'll rain later, so take a jacket with you.
## Mots intensifieurs
dead | grave, vachement (britannique) | The new restaurant is dead good, you should definitely go there.
way | largement, bien plus | This phone is way better than the one I had before.
wicked | terrible, canon (argot) | That concert last night was wicked, the crowd was amazing.
proper | un vrai de vrai, complet (britannique) | We had a proper English breakfast with beans, eggs and toast.
mega | super, méga | The queue for the new ride at the park was mega long.
hella | super, vachement (américain) | That road trip along the coast was hella fun, let's do it again.
flipping | putain de, fichu | The flipping printer has broken down again just before my deadline.
jolly | joliment, sacrément (britannique) | That was a jolly good effort, you should be proud of yourself.
ever so | on ne peut plus, extrêmement (britannique) | Thank you ever so much for looking after the cat while we were away.
a tad | un poil, un tout petit peu | The soup is a tad too salty, but otherwise it's delicious.
## Abréviations familières
gonna | aller faire (going to) | I'm gonna call my mum after work and tell her the news.
wanna | vouloir (want to) | Do you wanna come with us to the beach this afternoon?
gotta | devoir (got to) | Sorry, I've gotta go, my train leaves in fifteen minutes.
dunno | ne pas savoir (don't know) | I dunno where Omar put the keys, ask him when he gets back.
lemme | laisser (let me) | Lemme check my calendar before I promise anything for next week.
gimme | donner (give me) | Gimme a minute, I'm just finishing this email to the client.
y'all | vous tous (américain) | Are y'all coming to the family dinner on Sunday evening?
ain't | être pas, avoir pas (argot) | That ain't the way we do things around here, my friend.
innit | n'est-ce pas (britannique) | It's freezing outside today, innit? Better take a scarf.
cos | parce que (because) | I stayed home cos I wasn't feeling great this morning.
## Transitions et nuances conversationnelles
that reminds me | en parlant de ça, ça me rappelle | You mentioned Lisbon; that reminds me, Diego is moving there in May.
come to think of it | à bien y réfléchir | Come to think of it, I haven't seen Yuki at the gym for weeks.
long story short | bref, pour faire court | Long story short, we missed the ferry and spent the night on the island.
if you ask me | à mon avis, si tu veux mon avis | If you ask me, the old design looked much better than the new one.
to be fair | à vrai dire, honnêtement | To be fair, he did warn us that the road would be closed.
just saying | c'est tout ce que je dis | That jacket looked better on you than this one, just saying.
no offence | sans t'offenser, sans vouloir te vexer | No offence, but your cooking has been a bit salty lately.
between you and me | entre nous | Between you and me, I think Lukas is planning to quit next month.
for what it's worth | pour ce que ça vaut | For what it's worth, I think you made the right decision.
at the end of the day | tout compte fait, au bout du compte | At the end of the day, it's your money, so it's your choice.
