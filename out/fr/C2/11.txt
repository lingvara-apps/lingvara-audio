# UNIT: Verbes à particules III | Phrasal verbs avancés
## Conflit et recul
square up to | affronter, se mesurer à | The two players squared up to each other before the referee stepped in.
round on | s'en prendre à, agresser verbalement | When I asked about the money, Lukas suddenly rounded on me in front of everyone.
shout down | couvrir de cris, réduire au silence | Every time the speaker tried to answer, the angry crowd shouted him down.
back down | reculer, céder | Neither side was willing to back down, so the talks went on all night.
climb down | faire machine arrière, battre en retraite | After a week of protests, the management had to climb down and cancel the new rules.
have it out with (had, had) | s'expliquer avec, crever l'abcès | I finally had it out with my flatmate about the dirty dishes.
flare up | éclater, flamber | Old tensions between the two families flared up again at the wedding.
stir up | fomenter, provoquer | Someone has been stirring up trouble between the night shift and the day shift.
rub in | remuer le couteau dans la plaie | I know I lost the bet, Omar, there's no need to rub it in.
gang up on | s'allier contre, s'acharner sur | The older boys used to gang up on Diego after school.
## Tromperie et ruse
take in (took, taken) | berner, tromper | Even experienced collectors were taken in by the clever fake painting.
string along (strung, strung) | faire marcher, faire patienter | He strung her along for two years without ever planning to marry her.
palm off on | refourguer à, fourguer à | The dealer tried to palm off a broken watch on an unsuspecting tourist.
rip off | arnaquer, escroquer | Tourists often get ripped off by taxi drivers near the station.
cook up | inventer, fabriquer | Ali cooked up a story about a flat tyre to explain why he was late.
trump up | fabriquer de toutes pièces, inventer (de fausses accusations) | The journalist was arrested on trumped-up charges and released a month later.
cover up | étouffer, camoufler | The company tried to cover up the accident, but a worker told the press.
sell out (sold, sold) | se vendre, trahir ses principes | Fans accused the band of selling out when they started writing advertising songs.
put one over on (put, put) | berner, mystifier | Nobody puts one over on my grandmother, she checks every receipt twice.
play along | faire semblant, jouer le jeu | I knew it was a surprise party, but I played along and acted shocked.
## Persuasion et pression
goad into | pousser à bout, aiguillonner | His friends goaded him into jumping from the bridge into the cold river.
talk out of | dissuader de | Her friends tried to talk her out of quitting her job, but she had decided.
railroad into | bousculer pour faire accepter, forcer la main | Don't let the salesman railroad you into signing the contract today.
bully into | intimider pour forcer à | The workers were bullied into accepting longer hours without extra pay.
coax out of | amadouer pour obtenir, persuader en douceur | Yuki gently coaxed the frightened kitten out of its hiding place under the sofa.
butter up | passer de la pommade à, flatter | Emre has been buttering up his boss all week because he wants Friday off.
prevail upon | convaincre (soutenu), persuader | We prevailed upon the professor to give one more lecture before retiring.
hold out for (held, held) | tenir bon pour obtenir | The union is holding out for a ten percent pay rise.
jolly along | cajoler, mener par le bout du nez | The guide jollied the tired hikers along with jokes and promises of hot soup.
push for | faire pression pour, réclamer avec insistance | Local residents are pushing for a new crossing near the primary school.
## Émotions et humeur
choke up | avoir la gorge nouée, s'émouvoir | Lukas choked up halfway through his speech at his sister's wedding.
pine for | se languir de, regretter amèrement | Living abroad, Omar often pined for his mother's cooking and the noise of home.
perk up | se redresser, reprendre des couleurs | Emre perked up immediately when he heard there was cake in the kitchen.
buck up | se ressaisir, reprendre courage | Buck up, Sofia, one bad exam result isn't the end of the world.
hole up | se terrer, s'isoler | After the break-up, he holed up in his flat and refused to see anyone.
simmer down | se calmer, redescendre | Simmer down, everyone, we can discuss this calmly after lunch.
lighten up | se détendre, décompresser | Lighten up, it was only a joke and nobody meant to upset you.
psych up | se conditionner, se motiver | The team psyched themselves up with loud music before the big match.
boil over | déborder, tourner à l'aigre | Months of frustration finally boiled over into a furious argument.
tense up | se crisper, se tendre | Ali tenses up whenever someone asks him about his exam results.
## Argent et comptes
stump up | cracher au bassinet, casquer (familier) | The parents had to stump up another hundred euros for the school trip.
cough up | cracher (l'argent), allonger (familier) | Come on, Lukas, cough up, you still owe me for the concert tickets.
tot up | totaliser, additionner | Camille totted up the bill and was shocked by how much we had spent.
rack up | accumuler, engranger | Within a year he had racked up huge debts on three different credit cards.
siphon off | détourner (de l'argent), siphoner | The manager had been siphoning off company funds into a private account.
salt away | mettre de côté, thésauriser | Over the years she had salted away enough money to buy a small cottage.
squirrel away | planquer, amasser en cachette | My grandfather squirrelled away cash in old biscuit tins all over the house.
buy out (bought, bought) | racheter les parts de | Anna bought out her business partner and now runs the bakery alone.
buy up (bought, bought) | acheter en bloc, raffler | Investors are buying up old houses in the village and turning them into hotels.
mark down | brader, marquer à prix réduit | All the winter coats have been marked down by forty percent.
## Compréhension et apprentissage
puzzle out | décortiquer, élucider | It took us an hour to puzzle out the instructions for the new printer.
chew over | ressasser, mûrir (une idée) | Let me chew it over this weekend and I'll give you an answer on Monday.
sink in | faire son chemin, imprimer (les esprits) | It took a few days for the news of her promotion to sink in.
hit upon (hit, hit) | avoir une illumination, trouver par hasard | While washing the dishes, Omar hit upon a brilliant name for the café.
latch onto | s'accrocher à, focaliser sur | The media latched onto one careless remark and ignored the rest of the speech.
cotton on | percuter, réaliser (familier) | It took me ages to cotton on that they were planning a surprise for me.
catch on (caught, caught) | faire un tabac, devenir populaire | The new dance caught on quickly and soon everyone at school was doing it.
bone up on | réviser, bûcher (familier) | Before the interview, Diego boned up on the company's recent projects.
swot up on | bachoter, bosser dur | Yuki spent the whole weekend swotting up on chemistry formulas.
wise up | ouvrir les yeux, réaliser enfin | It's time you wised up and realised that he's never going to change.
## Styles de parole
rattle off | débiter, égrener à toute vitesse | The waiter rattled off a list of twelve desserts without taking a breath.
trail off | s'éteindre, retomber en fin de phrase | Her voice trailed off as she noticed everyone was staring at her.
pipe up | l'ouvrir, intervenir soudainement | A small boy at the back piped up with a surprisingly clever question.
harp on | rabâcher, ressasser sans cesse | My uncle keeps harping on about how cheap everything was in his day.
drone on | parler d'une voix monocorde, pontifier | The lecturer droned on for two hours and half the students fell asleep.
rabbit on | jacasser, bavarder sans fin | Sofia was rabbiting on about her holiday and didn't notice I was bored.
clam up | se fermer comme une huître, s'emurer dans le silence | Whenever we ask about his past, Lukas clams up and changes the subject.
sound off | pérorer, l'ouvrir fort | He loves sounding off about politics at family dinners.
mouth off | l'ouvrir grossièrement, jacasser avec arrogance | The player was sent off for mouthing off at the referee.
gush over | s'extasier devant, tarir d'éloges | All the relatives gushed over the new baby's tiny hands and feet.
## Évitement et fuite des responsabilités
duck out of | se défiler, esquiver | Diego always manages to duck out of washing up after dinner.
cop out | se dérober, choisir la solution de facilité | Saying you're too busy is just a way of copping out of the decision.
chicken out | se dégonfler, reculer par peur | Anna was going to do a bungee jump, but she chickened out at the last minute.
shy away from | reculer devant, esquiver | Good leaders don't shy away from difficult conversations with their teams.
shrink from (shrank, shrunk) | rechigner à, fuir | She never shrank from telling the truth, even when it was painful.
steer clear of | se tenir à l'écart de, éviter | If I were you, I'd steer clear of that restaurant, the fish isn't fresh.
weasel out of | se soustraire par la ruse, esquiver | He always tries to weasel out of paying his share of the bill.
beg off | se désister, décliner une invitation | Camille begged off the dinner, saying she had a terrible headache.
pull out | se retirer, faire marche arrière | The sponsor pulled out of the festival just two weeks before it opened.
back away from | faire machine arrière face à, se distancier de | The government has backed away from its promise to build new hospitals.
## Persévérance et effort
plough on | foncer malgré tout, continuer obstinément | Despite the rain, the volunteers ploughed on until the whole beach was clean.
press ahead | aller de l'avant, foncer | The city is pressing ahead with plans for a new tram line.
soldier on | serrer les dents, continuer envers et contre tout | Although she had a cold, Maria soldiered on and finished the report.
plug away at | bûcher régulièrement, s'accrocher à | Emre has been plugging away at his novel every evening for three years.
slog away | trimer, bosser dur | We slogged away in the hot kitchen from early morning until midnight.
buckle down | s'y mettre sérieusement, retrousser ses manches | If you want to pass, you'll need to buckle down and start revising now.
forge ahead | progresser à pas de géant, foncer | The small start-up forged ahead and soon overtook its bigger rivals.
pitch in | mettre la main à la pâte, participer | When the storm damaged the school, the whole village pitched in to repair it.
step up | monter au créneau, intensifier | When the captain was injured, the youngest player stepped up and scored twice.
see through (saw, seen) | mener à bien, aller jusqu'au bout | It's a difficult project, but Yuki is determined to see it through.
## Apparition et dénouement
spring up (sprang, sprung) | surgir, pousser comme des champignons | New coffee shops have sprung up all over the neighbourhood this year.
fall through (fell, fallen) | tomber à l'eau, échouer | Our plans to buy the flat fell through when the bank refused the loan.
dry up | se tarir, s'assécher | After the factory closed, work in the town completely dried up.
wear off (wore, worn) | s'estomper, s'atténuer | The painkillers started to wear off just as the film reached its climax.
boil down to | se résumer à, se réduire à | In the end, the whole argument boils down to a question of money.
pan out | tourner, se solder | Let's wait and see how things pan out before we make any decisions.
play out | se dérouler, se jouer | The drama played out in front of millions of viewers on live television.
round off | couronner, parachever | We rounded off the evening with a walk along the harbour.
polish off | s'enfiler, engloutir rapidement | The kids polished off the whole pizza in less than five minutes.
shoot up (shot, shot) | monter en flèche, s'envoler | Rents in the city centre have shot up since the new metro line opened.
