# UNIT: Expressions idiomatiques III : le corps | Expressions avec l'oreille, l'œil, le cœur, la langue, la main, le pied, la tête
## L'œil
give someone the evil eye | jeter un mauvais œil à quelqu'un | The old man gave us the evil eye when our ball landed in his garden.
with one's eyes open | en toute connaissance de cause | Ali took the risky job with his eyes open, knowing the pay was low at first.
pull the wool over someone's eyes | jeter de la poudre aux yeux de quelqu'un | Don't try to pull the wool over my eyes, I know you didn't do your homework.
have an eye for | avoir l'œil pour | Camille has an eye for colour, so ask her to choose the curtains.
make eyes at | faire de l'œil à quelqu'un | The boy at the next table kept making eyes at Sofia all evening.
only have eyes for | n'avoir d'yeux que pour | There were many guests at the party, but Diego only had eyes for Ana.
not believe one's eyes | n'en croire ses yeux | When I saw the price of the tickets, I couldn't believe my eyes.
one's eyes are bigger than one's stomach | avoir les yeux plus gros que le ventre | Lukas filled three plates at the buffet, but his eyes were bigger than his stomach.
raise eyebrows | faire froncer les sourcils | His decision to wear trainers to the wedding raised a few eyebrows.
up to one's eyes | jusqu'au cou | I'd love to help, but I'm up to my eyes in paperwork this week.
## L'oreille
lend an ear | prêter l'oreille | If you ever need someone to talk to, I'm always happy to lend an ear.
fall on deaf ears | tomber dans l'oreille d'un sourd | Our complaints about the noise fell on deaf ears for months.
keep one's ear to the ground | avoir l'oreille tendue | Keep your ear to the ground and tell me if you hear about any job openings.
have someone's ear | avoir l'oreille de quelqu'un | Omar has the director's ear, so ask him to mention your idea.
one's ears are burning | avoir les oreilles qui sifflent | Were your ears burning last night, Lukas? We were all talking about you.
grin from ear to ear | sourire jusqu'aux oreilles | When Emre saw his exam results, he was grinning from ear to ear.
wet behind the ears | né de la dernière pluie | The new manager is a bit wet behind the ears, but he learns quickly.
music to one's ears | de la musique pour les oreilles | When the boss said we could leave early, it was music to my ears.
go in one ear and out the other | entrer par une oreille et sortir par l'autre | I tell my son to tidy his room, but it goes in one ear and out the other.
prick up one's ears | dresser l'oreille | The dog pricked up its ears at the sound of the car in the drive.
## Le cœur
one's heart goes out to | avoir le cœur serré pour | Our hearts go out to all the families who lost their homes in the flood.
pour one's heart out | ouvrir son cœur | Late at night, Emre poured his heart out to his oldest friend.
with a heavy heart | le cœur gros | With a heavy heart, the family decided to sell the old farm.
heart and soul | corps et âme | She put her heart and soul into building the village library.
close to one's heart | tenir à cœur | Protecting the local forest is a cause very close to my heart.
not have the heart to | n'avoir pas le cœur à | I didn't have the heart to tell him that his singing was terrible.
a heart of stone | un cœur de pierre | Only someone with a heart of stone could ignore a crying child.
one's heart skips a beat | le cœur rate un battement | My heart skipped a beat when I saw her name on the list of winners.
take heart | prendre courage | Take heart from the fact that the second half of the course is much easier.
to one's heart's content | à cœur joie | At the seaside, the children could swim and play to their hearts' content.
## La langue, la bouche et les lèvres
set tongues wagging | faire aller les langues | Their late-night walks along the beach soon set tongues wagging in the village.
get one's tongue round | arriver à prononcer | I still can't get my tongue round the name of that Welsh village.
button one's lip | tenir sa langue | Button your lip, Omar, the surprise will be ruined if you say anything.
have a big mouth | avoir un grand bavoir | Don't tell Lukas your secret, he has a big mouth.
keep one's mouth shut | tenir sa langue [fermer sa bouche] | If anyone asks about the plan, just keep your mouth shut.
foam at the mouth | écumer de rage | The coach was foaming at the mouth after the referee's terrible decision.
my lips are sealed | mon secret est bien gardé | Don't worry about the surprise party, my lips are sealed.
lick one's lips | se lécher les babines | The fans were licking their lips at the thought of the final.
tongue-tied | avoir la langue nouée | Anna was so nervous at the interview that she became completely tongue-tied.
put words into someone's mouth | faire dire ce qu'on n'a pas dit | Don't put words into my mouth, I never said the plan was bad.
## La main
hand over fist | à flots continus | During the summer season, the beach cafés make money hand over fist.
sit on one's hands | rester les bras croisés | The council sat on its hands while the old theatre slowly fell apart.
show one's hand | abattre ses cartes | The buyer refused to show his hand until he had seen all the offers.
force someone's hand | forcer la main à quelqu'un | The leaked email forced the director's hand, and she resigned the next day.
know something like the back of one's hand | connaître comme sa poche | Ali grew up here, so he knows these streets like the back of his hand.
an old hand | un vieux routier | Ask Omar to fix it, he's an old hand at repairing bicycles.
a free hand | les mains libres | The new chef was given a free hand to change the whole menu.
hands down | haut la main | This is hands down the best soup I have ever tasted.
hand someone something on a plate | servir sur un plateau | The defender's mistake handed the other team the victory on a plate.
live from hand to mouth | vivre au jour le jour | As young artists, they lived from hand to mouth in a tiny flat in Dublin.
## Le pied et la jambe
put one's best foot forward | mettre son meilleur profil | On your first day at work, try to put your best foot forward.
have one foot in the grave | avoir un pied dans la tombe | My grandfather jokes that he has one foot in the grave, but he still swims every day.
have itchy feet | avoir la bougeotte | After two years in the same city, Yuki had itchy feet again.
never set foot in | ne jamais mettre les pieds à | After the way they treated me, I'll never set foot in that shop again.
be rushed off one's feet | être débordé | The nurses were rushed off their feet during the winter flu season.
on one's last legs | rendre l'âme | My old car is on its last legs, I'll have to buy a new one soon.
have two left feet | avoir deux pieds gauches | I'd love to dance with you, but I'm afraid I have two left feet.
think on one's feet | réagir au quart de tour | Good teachers need to think on their feet when a lesson goes wrong.
not have a leg to stand on | n'avoir aucun moyen de se défendre | Without a written contract, you don't have a leg to stand on.
sweep someone off their feet | faire chavirer le cœur de quelqu'un | He swept her off her feet with his charm and his terrible jokes.
## La tête
laugh one's head off | rire à s'en décrocher la mâchoire | The children laughed their heads off at the clown's silly tricks.
put heads together | se mettre à plusieurs | If we put our heads together, I'm sure we'll find a solution.
heads will roll | des têtes vont tomber | When the boss finds out about the lost files, heads will roll.
come to a head | arriver à un point critique | The dispute between the neighbours came to a head when one of them called the police.
need one's head examined | avoir un grain | You want to swim in the sea in January? You need your head examined!
bite someone's head off | envoyer promener | I only asked if dinner was ready, there's no need to bite my head off!
can't make head nor tail of | n'y comprendre que pouic | I can't make head nor tail of these instructions for the new washing machine.
go to one's head | monter à la tête | Don't let all this praise go to your head, you still have a lot to learn.
head and shoulders above | de loin supérieur | As a singer, she is head and shoulders above the other contestants.
bang one's head against a brick wall | parler à un mur | Trying to change the director's mind is like banging your head against a brick wall.
## Le visage et le nez
keep one's nose clean | se tenir à carreau | After his release, Diego kept his nose clean and found a steady job.
a slap in the face | une gifle | Being passed over for the promotion was a real slap in the face for Anna.
keep a straight face | garder son sérieux | When he walked in wearing that hat, I could hardly keep a straight face.
save face | sauver la face | To save face, the company called the failed product a test version.
face the music | assumer les conséquences | You broke the window, Lukas, so now you'll have to face the music.
pull a long face | faire une mine de six pieds de long | There's no point pulling a long face, we're not buying another video game.
fly in the face of | aller à l'encontre de | His theory flies in the face of everything we know about the climate.
turn one's nose up at | faire la fine bouche | My nephew turns his nose up at any vegetable that is green.
under someone's nose | sous le nez | The keys were right under my nose the whole time I was searching.
poke one's nose into | fourrer son nez dans | I wish our neighbour would stop poking her nose into our business.
## L'épaule, le dos, le cou et le bras
a chip on one's shoulder | une rancœur tenace | He has a chip on his shoulder because he never went to university.
behind someone's back | dans le dos de quelqu'un | I can't believe they were laughing at me behind my back.
breathe down someone's neck | souffler dans le cou | I can't concentrate with my manager breathing down my neck all day.
keep someone at arm's length | tenir à distance | Sofia is friendly, but she keeps new colleagues at arm's length.
turn one's back on | tourner le dos à | He turned his back on a successful career in law to become a farmer.
rub shoulders with | coudoyer | At the film festival, young directors rub shoulders with famous actors.
get someone's back up | dresser les cheveux sur la tête | His arrogant tone really gets my back up.
put one's back into it | mettre le paquet | If you put your back into it, we'll finish painting the fence by lunchtime.
neck and neck | coude à coude | The two runners were neck and neck until the very last metre.
twist someone's arm | tordre le bras à quelqu'un | I didn't want another dessert, but Omar twisted my arm.
## Le doigt, la dent et l'os
point the finger at | pointer du doigt | When the project failed, everyone pointed the finger at the new manager.
have a finger in every pie | être de tous les combats | Our neighbour has a finger in every pie, from the school board to the local choir.
all fingers and thumbs | avoir deux mains gauches | I'm all fingers and thumbs today, I've already dropped two cups.
not lift a finger | ne pas lever le petit doigt | My brother didn't lift a finger while we were cleaning the house.
wrap someone round one's little finger | mener par le bout du nez | Little Sofia has wrapped her grandfather round her little finger.
by the skin of one's teeth | de justesse | We caught the last train by the skin of our teeth.
lie through one's teeth | mentir effrontément | He said he'd never seen the money, but he was lying through his teeth.
long in the tooth | vieux jeu | I'm getting a bit long in the tooth to sleep in a tent.
have a bone to pick with | avoir un compte à régler | I have a bone to pick with you, Diego, why did you eat my sandwich?
make no bones about | ne pas mâcher ses mots | Camille made no bones about her dislike of the new design.
