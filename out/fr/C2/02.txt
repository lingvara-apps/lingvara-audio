# UNIT: Nuances des verbes synonymes | Regarder, parler, marcher, rire... nuances subtiles
## Regarder
gaze | contempler, fixer longuement | Maria sat by the window, gazing at the rain for nearly an hour.
stare | fixer (des yeux), dévisager | The little girl stared at the giraffe as if she couldn't believe her eyes.
peer | lorgner, scruter en plissant les yeux | Diego peered into the dark cellar, trying to find the light switch.
gawk | loucher, regarder bêtement | Drivers slowed down to gawk at the accident on the other side.
ogle | lorgner avec insistance, reluquer | He spent the whole evening ogling the expensive cars outside the hotel.
goggle | écarquiller les yeux, regarder fixement | The children goggled at the magician as the rabbit vanished from his hat.
peek | jeter un coup d'œil, regarder en cachette | The children peeked through the curtains to see if the guests had arrived.
rubberneck | regarder par curiosité, tourner la tête pour voir | Traffic slowed to a crawl as drivers rubbernecked at the burning lorry.
gape | rester bouche bée, contempler béatement | The tourists stood gaping at the enormous waterfall.
eyeball | toiser, regarder fixement | The two rival players eyeballed each other before the match began.
## Voir et remarquer
glimpse | entrapercevoir, apercevoir brièvement | We glimpsed the castle through the fog before it disappeared again.
discern | discerner, distinguer | In the dim light, it was hard to discern any faces in the crowd.
perceive | percevoir, considérer | Many patients perceive the new treatment as less painful.
detect | détecter, déceler | Yuki detected a slight note of sarcasm in his reply.
catch sight of | apercevoir, attraper du regard | Emre caught sight of his old teacher across the busy station.
make out | distinguer, deviner | I could just make out the shape of a boat on the horizon.
scan | scruter, balayer du regard | Sofia scanned the room for a familiar face before sitting down.
skim | parcourir rapidement, survoler | I only had time to skim the report on the train this morning.
peruse | lire attentivement, examiner | Lukas perused the wine list for a long time before ordering water.
spy | apercevoir de loin, repérer | From the hilltop, Omar spied a small cottage hidden among the trees.
## Murmure et bavardage
whisper | chuchoter, murmurer | Emre leaned over and whispered the answer to his classmate.
chatter | bavarder, jaser | The two friends chattered away happily on the long train journey.
gabble | baragouiner, parler à toute vitesse | In her excitement, Sofia gabbled the whole story in about ten seconds.
jabber | baragouiner, jacasser | The tourists were jabbering in a language none of us could recognise.
stutter | bégayer | As a child, Ali stuttered, but speech therapy helped him enormously.
babble | babiller, baragouiner | The baby babbled happily while her parents cooked dinner.
blather | radoter, dire des inepties | He blathered on about his new car while everyone else looked bored.
lisp | zézayer | The little boy lisped a little when he tried to say his sister's name.
prattle | jacasser, bavarder sans fin | She prattled on about her holiday while we tried to watch the film.
witter | radoter, bavarder vainement | My neighbour wittered on about the weather for twenty minutes.
## Crier et grogner
yell | crier, hurler | The coach yelled instructions from the side of the pitch.
squeal | pousser un cri aigu, grincer | The tyres squealed as the car turned the corner too fast.
roar | rugir, hurler | The crowd roared when the winning goal went in.
shriek | pousser un cri perçant, hurler | The children shrieked with delight as the waves splashed them.
screech | crier, pousser un cri strident | A parrot screeched from its cage every time the doorbell rang.
squawk | croasser, rouspéter bruyamment | The passengers squawked about the delay, but nothing could be done.
howl | hurler, mugir | The wind howled around the house all night long.
hiss | siffler | "Be quiet," she hissed. "They'll hear us!"
growl | grogner | "Get out of my office," the director growled without looking up.
snarl | grommeler, montrer les dents | The tired waiter snarled at a customer who complained about the soup.
## Marcher
stroll | flâner, se promener | On Sunday evenings, couples stroll along the seafront.
wander | errer, flâner | We wandered through the old town with no particular plan.
roam | errer, vagabonder | Herds of wild ponies roam freely across the moors.
stride | marcher à grands pas | Camille strode confidently across the stage to accept the award.
lumber | marcher lourdement, se traîner | A huge bear lumbered out of the forest towards the river.
pace | faire les cent pas | Omar paced up and down the corridor while waiting for news.
tramp | marcher d'un pas lourd, battre le pavé | They tramped across the muddy fields for hours in the rain.
wade | marcher dans l'eau, guéer | The fishermen waded into the river up to their knees.
limp | boiter | Emre limped off the pitch after twisting his ankle.
dawdle | flâner, traîner | Stop dawdling or we'll miss the start of the film!
## Courir et se dépêcher
dash | se précipiter, foncer | Maria dashed out of the house when she heard the bus coming.
bolt | s'enfuir en trombe, détaler | The horse bolted when a car horn sounded behind it.
race | courir à toute vitesse, filer | Anna raced up the stairs to answer the phone.
streak | passer en trombe, fuser | A fox streaked across the road in front of our car.
hurtle | dévaler à toute allure, foncer | A huge rock hurtled down the mountain towards the road.
dart | fuser, s'élancer comme une flèche | A small fish darted out from behind the coral.
scoot | filer, décamper | I'll just scoot down to the shop before it closes.
hasten | se hâter, se dépêcher | The waiter hastened to apologise when he saw the broken glass.
zoom | vrombir, filer à toute vitesse | Delivery scooters zoomed past us on the narrow street.
gallop | galoper | The riders galloped across the open field at sunrise.
## Rire et sourire
giggle | glousser, ricaner | The girls giggled at the back of the classroom during the lesson.
chuckle | rire sous cape, glousser | Omar chuckled quietly as he read the funny message.
grin | sourire jusqu'aux oreilles, s'en donner à cœur joie | Ali grinned from ear to ear when he heard he had passed.
chortle | pousser un rire étouffé, rigoler | Grandpa chortled at his own joke before he even finished it.
hoot | éclater de rire | The audience hooted with laughter at the comedian's first joke.
cackle | rire d'un rire grinçant, kakarder | The witch in the play cackled as she stirred her pot.
crease up | plier de rire, se fendre la poire | His impression of the headteacher had us all creasing up.
be in stitches | être mort de rire, se tordre de rire | The film was so funny that we were in stitches the whole time.
split one's sides | se fendre la poire, rire aux éclats | The children split their sides laughing at the clown's tricks.
fall about laughing | s'écrouler de rire, se poiler | When Lukas fell into the pool fully dressed, we fell about laughing.
## Pleurer
sob | sangloter | Sofia sobbed on her friend's shoulder after hearing the news.
weep (wept, wept) | pleurer, verser des larmes | The old man wept silently at his wife's grave.
tear up | avoir les larmes aux yeux, s'émouvoir | Camille teared up when her students sang her a goodbye song.
keen | entonner une complainte, gémir | In the old story, the women keened over the fallen hero all night.
bawl | pleurer à chaudes larmes, brailler | The toddler bawled because his ice cream had fallen on the ground.
blubber | chialer, pleurnicher | Stop blubbering and tell me what actually happened.
mewl | miauler, geindre | A hungry kitten mewled in the box by the door.
snivel | pleurnicher, renifler | He snivelled about losing the game instead of congratulating the winner.
cry one's eyes out | pleurer toutes les larmes de son corps | Maria cried her eyes out when her old dog died.
choke back tears | retenir ses larmes | Diego choked back tears as he said goodbye to his team.
## Tenir, attraper et tirer
grab | attraper, saisir | Diego grabbed his coat and ran out to catch the train.
seize | s'emparer de, saisir | The guard seized the thief by the arm before he could escape.
snatch | arracher, subtiliser | A seagull snatched the sandwich right out of my hand.
grasp | saisir, empoigner | She grasped the rope with both hands and started to climb.
grip | agripper, serrer fermement | The nervous passenger gripped the armrest during the landing.
clasp | serrer dans ses mains, enlacer | The two old friends clasped hands warmly at the airport.
cling (clung, clung) | s'accrocher, se cramponner | The frightened child clung to her mother's leg in the crowd.
yank | donner un coup sec, tirer brusquement | Omar yanked the door open and shouted for help.
tug | tirer légèrement, secouer | The little boy tugged at his father's sleeve to get his attention.
haul | haler, traîner avec effort | The fishermen hauled their heavy nets onto the deck.
## Manger et boire
gobble | engloutir, se goinfrer | The kids gobbled their dinner so they could go back outside.
devour | dévorer, engloutir | The hungry hikers devoured every piece of bread on the table.
nibble | grignoter | Camille nibbled a biscuit while she waited for the results.
munch | mastiquer bruyamment, croquer | He sat on the sofa munching crisps during the whole film.
wolf down | avaler goulûment, dévorer | Ali wolfed down his breakfast and rushed to the office.
gulp | avaler d'un trait, engloutir | She gulped her water after the long run in the heat.
sip | siroter | Anna sipped her tea slowly on the balcony.
slurp | slurper, manger ou boire bruyamment | It's considered rude to slurp your soup in many countries.
swig | avaler à grandes gorgées, caler | The cyclist swigged from his bottle without stopping.
guzzle | s'empiffrer de boissons, boire goulûment | Old cars guzzle fuel, so many people are switching to electric ones.
