# UNIT: Palavras antigas e literárias | A linguagem arcaica da poesia e da literatura clássica
## Corpo e aparência (linguagem antiga)
visage | rosto, semblante (literário) | Her pale visage appeared at the window like a ghost in the moonlight.
countenance | semblante, expressão facial | The old king's countenance darkened when he heard the news.
brow | testa (literário) | A deep frown furrowed the scholar's brow as he read the letter.
bosom | seio, peito (literário) | She held the child close to her bosom through the cold night.
tresses | longas madeixas, cachos (poético) | Her golden tresses tumbled over her shoulders in the morning wind.
sinew | tendão, fibra muscular (literário) | Every sinew in his body strained as he pulled the boat ashore.
orb | olho; esfera (poético) | Her bright blue orbs were filled with tears of joy.
mien | porte, postura, ar | The stranger had the proud mien of a soldier who had seen many wars.
raiment | vestimenta, veste (antigo) | The priestess appeared in white raiment embroidered with silver thread.
apparel | vestuário, roupa (formal, antigo) | The nobles arrived at the feast in their finest apparel.
## Natureza (poético)
vale | vale (poético) | A gentle mist lay over the quiet vale at dawn.
dale | pequeno vale, ravina (poético) | The shepherd led his flock over hill and dale all summer.
brook | riacho, córrego | We sat beside a babbling brook and listened to the birds.
mere | lago, pântano (poético) | Swans glided silently across the still surface of the mere.
firmament | firmamento, abóbada celeste | Countless stars glittered across the dark firmament above the sea.
zephyr | brisa leve, zéfiro | A warm zephyr carried the scent of roses into the garden.
gloaming | crepúsculo (poético) | They walked home slowly in the soft gloaming of a summer evening.
bower | caramanchão, pérgula, bosque | The lovers met secretly in a shady bower of roses.
sward | relvado, gramado (poético) | Children danced barefoot on the green sward by the river.
brine | mar, água salgada | The old sailor had spent forty years upon the brine.
## Pessoas (antigo)
maiden | donzela, moça (antigo) | A young maiden stood waiting by the castle gate.
damsel | donzela, jovem dama (antigo) | In the old tales, a brave knight always rescues the damsel.
knave | patife, bribão (antigo) | That lying knave has stolen the bishop's silver cup!
swain | rapaz campestre; jovem apaixonado (poético) | A lovesick swain sang beneath her window every night.
liege | senhor feudal, soberano | I swear to serve you faithfully, my liege, until my death.
kinsman | parente, consanguíneo (masculino, antigo) | He was welcomed at the castle as a kinsman of the lord.
churl | homem grosseiro, camponês (antigo) | Only a churl would refuse bread to a hungry traveller.
crone | bruxa, velha caduca (conto de fadas) | An old crone by the fire told the children strange stories.
sire | alteza, senhor (tratamento) | The enemy army is at the gates, sire. What shall we do?
minstrel | menestrel, trovador | A wandering minstrel entertained the guests with songs of old battles.
## Pronomes e formas verbais antigas
thou | tu (antigo, sujeito) | Thou shalt not pass this bridge without paying the toll.
thee | ti, te (antigo) | I have loved thee since the first day we met.
thy | teu, tua (antigo) | Speak softly, for thy words may wake the sleeping child.
thine | teu, tua; o teu (diante de vogal, antigo) | This land was once thine, but now it belongs to another.
ye | vós (antigo) | Hear me, ye people of the village, for I bring news.
thou art | tu és (antigo) | Thou art the fairest flower in all the kingdom.
hath | tem, possui (terceira pessoa, antigo) | The storm hath destroyed the harvest of the whole valley.
doth | faz (terceira pessoa, antigo) | The lady doth protest too much, methinks.
shalt | deves, -ás (futuro, antigo) | Thou shalt honour thy father and thy mother all thy days.
methinks | parece-me, julgo eu (antigo) | Methinks the stranger knows more than he is telling us.
## Advérbios antigos
hither | para cá, cá (antigo) | Come hither, child, and let me look at your face.
thither | para lá, lá (antigo) | The pilgrims travelled thither on foot from distant lands.
whither | para onde (antigo) | Whither are you going so late at night, good sir?
thence | dali, de lá (antigo) | They sailed to Lisbon and thence across the ocean to the New World.
yonder | acolá, lá longe | Do you see that light shining from yonder window?
anon | logo mais, daqui a pouco (antigo) | I must leave you now, but I shall return anon.
ere | antes de (antigo) | We must reach the castle ere the sun goes down.
oft | frequentemente, muitas vezes (poético) | The old songs are oft forgotten by the young.
betwixt | entre (antigo) | The little cottage stood betwixt the forest and the sea.
wherefore | por que, pelo que (antigo) | Wherefore do you weep, my friend, when the battle is won?
## Verbos antigos
behold (beheld, beheld) | contemplar, avistar | The travellers stopped to behold the sunrise over the mountains.
beseech (besought, besought) | implorar, suplicar | I beseech you, my lord, to spare my son's life.
smite (smote, smitten) | golpear violentamente, ferir (antigo) | The hero smote the giant with a single blow of his sword.
slay (slew, slain) | matar, assassinar (literário) | According to legend, the young prince slew the dragon alone.
hearken | escutar, dar ouvidos (antigo) | Hearken to the words of the wise, and you will prosper.
tarry | demorar-se, tardar (antigo) | Do not tarry in the forest after dark, for wolves are near.
wend | seguir caminho, marchar (antigo) | The weary soldiers wended their way home after the long war.
quoth | disse (antigo) | "I shall not yield," quoth the knight, raising his shield.
forsake (forsook, forsaken) | abandonar, desamparar | She swore she would never forsake her homeland.
cleave (clove, cloven) | fender, rachar | With one stroke of his axe he clove the log in two.
## Verbos de alto estilo
beget (begot, begotten) | gerar, dar origem a | Violence, as the old saying goes, only begets more violence.
espy | avistar de longe, divisar | From the tower the guard espied a rider approaching the town.
bewail | lamentar, prantear | The villagers bewailed the loss of their fishing boats in the storm.
rue | arrepender-se de, lamentar | You will rue the day you insulted the queen's champion.
vanquish | vencer, derrotar | The small army managed to vanquish a far greater enemy.
succour | socorrer, ajudar | The monks succoured the sick and the poor throughout the winter.
beguile | enfeitiçar, seduzir; enganar | The sailors were beguiled by the strange song coming from the rocks.
betroth | noivar, comprometer em casamento (antigo) | The princess was betrothed to a prince from a distant kingdom.
sunder | separar, romper (literário) | No power on earth shall sunder these two loving hearts.
rend (rent, rent) | rasgar, despedaçar (literário) | In her grief she rent her garments and wept aloud.
## Sociedade antiga
alms | esmola | Every Friday the merchant gave alms to the beggars at the gate.
tithe | dízimo | Peasants had to pay a tithe of their harvest to the church.
fealty | fidelidade, lealdade feudal | The knights swore fealty to the new king on their knees.
serf | servo, camponês preso à terra | A medieval serf could not leave the land without his lord's permission.
squire | escudeiro; fidalgo rural | The young squire carried his master's shield into battle.
steed | corcel, cavalo de guerra (literário) | The knight mounted his white steed and rode towards the castle.
troth | palavra de honra, compromisso (antigo) | At the altar, the young couple plighted their troth.
coffer | cofre, arca do tesouro | The king's coffers were empty after years of war.
bard | bardo, poeta | The bard sang of heroes and lost kingdoms by the fire.
scabbard | bainha | He slowly drew his sword from its leather scabbard.
## Nomes e adjetivos poéticos
woe | desgraça, angústia, dor | The poem tells a tale of love, loss and endless woe.
mirth | alegria, regozijo | The hall was filled with music, laughter and mirth.
bane | ruína, tormento, praga | That noisy rooster is the bane of my mornings.
guile | astúcia, artimanha, dolo | The fox won not by strength but by guile.
wondrous | maravilhoso, prodigioso (literário) | The travellers told wondrous tales of distant islands.
doughty | valente, corajoso (antigo) | The doughty old captain refused to abandon his ship.
hoary | grisalho, alvares; antiquíssimo | A hoary old man sat alone at the end of the bridge.
wan | pálido, esmaecido | After weeks of illness, her face looked thin and wan.
comely | formoso, atraente (antigo) | The miller had a comely daughter whom everyone admired.
blithe | alegre, despreocupado | She went on her way with a blithe and carefree heart.
## Interjeições e afirmações antigas
alas | ai de mim, infelizmente | Alas, the ship sank before it could reach the harbour.
prithee | por favor, peço-te (antigo) | Prithee, tell me the name of this fair village.
forsooth | decerto, ora vejam (antigo, irônico) | A great hero, forsooth, who runs at the sight of a mouse!
verily | verdadeiramente, em verdade (antigo) | Verily, I tell you, this man speaks the truth.
lo | eis, olhe (antigo) | And lo, a bright star appeared in the eastern sky.
fie | pafúncio, que vergonha (antigo) | Fie upon you for breaking your promise to the poor widow!
hark | ouve, escuta (antigo) | Hark, I hear the sound of horses on the road!
nay | não; mais ainda, antes (antigo) | It was a difficult, nay, an impossible journey through the mountains.
yea | sim (antigo) | Those who agree shall say yea, and those who disagree shall say nay.
perchance | talvez, porventura (antigo) | To sleep, perchance to dream, was all the weary traveller wished.
