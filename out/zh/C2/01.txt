# UNIT: 古词与文学词汇 | 诗歌与古典文学的古旧语汇
## 身体与外貌（古语）
visage | 面容，面颊（文） | Her pale visage appeared at the window like a ghost in the moonlight.
countenance | 面容，神情 | The old king's countenance darkened when he heard the news.
brow | 前额（文） | A deep frown furrowed the scholar's brow as he read the letter.
bosom | 胸怀，胸口（文） | She held the child close to her bosom through the cold night.
tresses | 长发，卷发（诗） | Her golden tresses tumbled over her shoulders in the morning wind.
sinew | 筋腱，肌肉力量（文） | Every sinew in his body strained as he pulled the boat ashore.
orb | 眼睛；球体（诗） | Her bright blue orbs were filled with tears of joy.
mien | 风度，仪态，神态 | The stranger had the proud mien of a soldier who had seen many wars.
raiment | 衣着，盛装（古） | The priestess appeared in white raiment embroidered with silver thread.
apparel | 服装，衣饰（正式，古） | The nobles arrived at the feast in their finest apparel.
## 自然（诗意）
vale | 山谷（诗） | A gentle mist lay over the quiet vale at dawn.
dale | 小谷，幽谷（诗） | The shepherd led his flock over hill and dale all summer.
brook | 小溪，溪流 | We sat beside a babbling brook and listened to the birds.
mere | 湖泊，泽地（诗） | Swans glided silently across the still surface of the mere.
firmament | 天穹，苍穹 | Countless stars glittered across the dark firmament above the sea.
zephyr | 和风，西风 | A warm zephyr carried the scent of roses into the garden.
gloaming | 薄暮，黄昏（诗） | They walked home slowly in the soft gloaming of a summer evening.
bower | 绿荫亭，树荫下 | The lovers met secretly in a shady bower of roses.
sward | 草地，草皮（诗） | Children danced barefoot on the green sward by the river.
brine | 海洋，盐水 | The old sailor had spent forty years upon the brine.
## 人物（古语）
maiden | 少女，处女（古） | A young maiden stood waiting by the castle gate.
damsel | 贵族少女，年轻女子（古） | In the old tales, a brave knight always rescues the damsel.
knave | 恶棍，流氓（古） | That lying knave has stolen the bishop's silver cup!
swain | 牧羊少年；情郎（诗） | A lovesick swain sang beneath her window every night.
liege | 封建领主，效忠对象 | I swear to serve you faithfully, my liege, until my death.
kinsman | 男亲属，族人（古） | He was welcomed at the castle as a kinsman of the lord.
churl | 粗野的人，乡下人（古） | Only a churl would refuse bread to a hungry traveller.
crone | 老太婆，巫婆（童话） | An old crone by the fire told the children strange stories.
sire | 陛下，大王（尊称） | The enemy army is at the gates, sire. What shall we do?
minstrel | 游吟诗人，歌唱艺人 | A wandering minstrel entertained the guests with songs of old battles.
## 古代代词与动词形式
thou | 你（古，主格） | Thou shalt not pass this bridge without paying the toll.
thee | 你（thou的宾格，古） | I have loved thee since the first day we met.
thy | 你的（古） | Speak softly, for thy words may wake the sleeping child.
thine | 你的；你的东西（元音前，古） | This land was once thine, but now it belongs to another.
ye | 你们（古） | Hear me, ye people of the village, for I bring news.
thou art | 你是（古） | Thou art the fairest flower in all the kingdom.
hath | 有，已经（have的第三人称单数，古） | The storm hath destroyed the harvest of the whole valley.
doth | 做，行（does的古体） | The lady doth protest too much, methinks.
shalt | 将要（shall的古体） | Thou shalt honour thy father and thy mother all thy days.
methinks | 我想，依我看（古） | Methinks the stranger knows more than he is telling us.
## 古代副词
hither | 到这里，向这里（古） | Come hither, child, and let me look at your face.
thither | 到那里，去那里（古） | The pilgrims travelled thither on foot from distant lands.
whither | 去哪里，何往（古） | Whither are you going so late at night, good sir?
thence | 从那里，自此（古） | They sailed to Lisbon and thence across the ocean to the New World.
yonder | 那里的，那边的 | Do you see that light shining from yonder window?
anon | 不久，一会儿（古） | I must leave you now, but I shall return anon.
ere | 在……之前（古） | We must reach the castle ere the sun goes down.
oft | 常常，经常（诗） | The old songs are oft forgotten by the young.
betwixt | 在……之间（古） | The little cottage stood betwixt the forest and the sea.
wherefore | 为什么，因此（古） | Wherefore do you weep, my friend, when the battle is won?
## 古代动词
behold (beheld, beheld) | 看见，目睹 | The travellers stopped to behold the sunrise over the mountains.
beseech (besought, besought) | 恳求，哀求 | I beseech you, my lord, to spare my son's life.
smite (smote, smitten) | 重击，猛打（古） | The hero smote the giant with a single blow of his sword.
slay (slew, slain) | 杀死，屠杀（文） | According to legend, the young prince slew the dragon alone.
hearken | 倾听，留心（古） | Hearken to the words of the wise, and you will prosper.
tarry | 逗留，耽搁（古） | Do not tarry in the forest after dark, for wolves are near.
wend | 行进，前行（古） | The weary soldiers wended their way home after the long war.
quoth | 说（古） | "I shall not yield," quoth the knight, raising his shield.
forsake (forsook, forsaken) | 抛弃，遗弃 | She swore she would never forsake her homeland.
cleave (clove, cloven) | 劈开，斩断 | With one stroke of his axe he clove the log in two.
## 高雅文风动词
beget (begot, begotten) | 产生，招致；生育 | Violence, as the old saying goes, only begets more violence.
espy | 远望，突然看见 | From the tower the guard espied a rider approaching the town.
bewail | 哀悼，痛哭 | The villagers bewailed the loss of their fishing boats in the storm.
rue | 后悔，悲叹 | You will rue the day you insulted the queen's champion.
vanquish | 战胜，征服 | The small army managed to vanquish a far greater enemy.
succour | 救援，救济 | The monks succoured the sick and the poor throughout the winter.
beguile | 诱惑，欺骗；使陶醉 | The sailors were beguiled by the strange song coming from the rocks.
betroth | 许配，订婚（古） | The princess was betrothed to a prince from a distant kingdom.
sunder | 分离，割断（文） | No power on earth shall sunder these two loving hearts.
rend (rent, rent) | 撕裂，猛扯（文） | In her grief she rent her garments and wept aloud.
## 古代社会
alms | 施舍物，救济金 | Every Friday the merchant gave alms to the beggars at the gate.
tithe | 十一税 | Peasants had to pay a tithe of their harvest to the church.
fealty | 效忠宣誓（封建） | The knights swore fealty to the new king on their knees.
serf | 农奴，佃农 | A medieval serf could not leave the land without his lord's permission.
squire | 骑士侍从；乡绅 | The young squire carried his master's shield into battle.
steed | 骏马，战马（文） | The knight mounted his white steed and rode towards the castle.
troth | 誓约，婚约（古） | At the altar, the young couple plighted their troth.
coffer | 保险箱，金库 | The king's coffers were empty after years of war.
bard | 吟游诗人，歌手 | The bard sang of heroes and lost kingdoms by the fire.
scabbard | 剑鞘，刀鞘 | He slowly drew his sword from its leather scabbard.
## 诗意名词与形容词
woe | 悲伤，苦难 | The poem tells a tale of love, loss and endless woe.
mirth | 欢乐，高兴 | The hall was filled with music, laughter and mirth.
bane | 祸根，苦恼之源 | That noisy rooster is the bane of my mornings.
guile | 诡计，狡诈 | The fox won not by strength but by guile.
wondrous | 奇妙的，惊人的（文） | The travellers told wondrous tales of distant islands.
doughty | 勇敢的，英勇的（古） | The doughty old captain refused to abandon his ship.
hoary | 白发苍苍的；古老的 | A hoary old man sat alone at the end of the bridge.
wan | 苍白的，病态的 | After weeks of illness, her face looked thin and wan.
comely | 清秀的，标致的（古） | The miller had a comely daughter whom everyone admired.
blithe | 快乐的，无忧无虑的 | She went on her way with a blithe and carefree heart.
## 古代感叹词与肯定词
alas | 哎呀，不幸的是 | Alas, the ship sank before it could reach the harbour.
prithee | 请，劳驾（古） | Prithee, tell me the name of this fair village.
forsooth | 确实，真的（古，讽刺） | A great hero, forsooth, who runs at the sight of a mouse!
verily | 确实，真的（古） | Verily, I tell you, this man speaks the truth.
lo | 看哪，瞧（古） | And lo, a bright star appeared in the eastern sky.
fie | 呸，耻辱（古） | Fie upon you for breaking your promise to the poor widow!
hark | 听，倾听（古） | Hark, I hear the sound of horses on the road!
nay | 不；甚至可以说（古） | It was a difficult, nay, an impossible journey through the mountains.
yea | 是的，甚至（古） | Those who agree shall say yea, and those who disagree shall say nay.
perchance | 也许，可能（古） | To sleep, perchance to dream, was all the weary traveller wished.
