# UNIT: Idiom III: tubuh | Ungkapan dengan telinga, mata, hati, lidah, tangan, kaki, dan kepala
## Mata
give someone the evil eye | memelototi; menatap tajam | The old man gave us the evil eye when our ball landed in his garden.
with one's eyes open | dengan sadar; mata terbuka | Ali took the risky job with his eyes open, knowing the pay was low at first.
pull the wool over someone's eyes | menipu; mengelabui | Don't try to pull the wool over my eyes, I know you didn't do your homework.
have an eye for | jeli dalam; pandai menilai | Camille has an eye for colour, so ask her to choose the curtains.
make eyes at | melirik-lirik; menggoda dengan mata | The boy at the next table kept making eyes at Sofia all evening.
only have eyes for | tidak melirik orang lain; hanya memerhatikan (satu orang) | There were many guests at the party, but Diego only had eyes for Ana.
not believe one's eyes | tidak percaya pada penglihatan sendiri | When I saw the price of the tickets, I couldn't believe my eyes.
one's eyes are bigger than one's stomach | serakah mengambil makanan; mata lapar | Lukas filled three plates at the buffet, but his eyes were bigger than his stomach.
raise eyebrows | memancing keheranan; membuat orang terkejut | His decision to wear trainers to the wedding raised a few eyebrows.
up to one's eyes | sibuk sekali; tenggelam dalam pekerjaan | I'd love to help, but I'm up to my eyes in paperwork this week.
## Telinga
lend an ear | mendengarkan; memerhatikan | If you ever need someone to talk to, I'm always happy to lend an ear.
fall on deaf ears | tidak didengar; diabaikan | Our complaints about the noise fell on deaf ears for months.
keep one's ear to the ground | waspada; memantau perkembangan | Keep your ear to the ground and tell me if you hear about any job openings.
have someone's ear | didengar (oleh orang penting); mendapat perhatian | Omar has the director's ear, so ask him to mention your idea.
one's ears are burning | telinga terasa panas; merasa dibicarakan orang | Were your ears burning last night, Lukas? We were all talking about you.
grin from ear to ear | tersenyum lebar | When Emre saw his exam results, he was grinning from ear to ear.
wet behind the ears | masih hijau; belum berpengalaman | The new manager is a bit wet behind the ears, but he learns quickly.
music to one's ears | kabar yang sangat menyenangkan | When the boss said we could leave early, it was music to my ears.
go in one ear and out the other | masuk telinga kiri keluar telinga kanan | I tell my son to tidy his room, but it goes in one ear and out the other.
prick up one's ears | memasang telinga; mendengarkan baik-baik | The dog pricked up its ears at the sound of the car in the drive.
## Hati
one's heart goes out to | merasa iba; berempati kepada | Our hearts go out to all the families who lost their homes in the flood.
pour one's heart out | mencurahkan isi hati | Late at night, Emre poured his heart out to his oldest friend.
with a heavy heart | dengan berat hati | With a heavy heart, the family decided to sell the old farm.
heart and soul | dengan segenap jiwa raga; sepenuh hati | She put her heart and soul into building the village library.
close to one's heart | sangat berarti; dekat di hati | Protecting the local forest is a cause very close to my heart.
not have the heart to | tidak tega; tidak sampai hati | I didn't have the heart to tell him that his singing was terrible.
a heart of stone | berhati batu; kejam | Only someone with a heart of stone could ignore a crying child.
one's heart skips a beat | jantung berdegup kencang karena kaget | My heart skipped a beat when I saw her name on the list of winners.
take heart | berbesar hati; mengambil semangat | Take heart from the fact that the second half of the course is much easier.
to one's heart's content | sepuas hati | At the seaside, the children could swim and play to their hearts' content.
## Lidah dan mulut
set tongues wagging | memicu gosip; menjadi bahan perbincangan | Their late-night walks along the beach soon set tongues wagging in the village.
get one's tongue round | bisa melafalkan (kata yang sulit) | I still can't get my tongue round the name of that Welsh village.
button one's lip | menjaga mulut; diam | Button your lip, Omar, the surprise will be ruined if you say anything.
have a big mouth | ember; suka membocorkan rahasia | Don't tell Lukas your secret, he has a big mouth.
keep one's mouth shut | menutup rapat mulut; diam | If anyone asks about the plan, just keep your mouth shut.
foam at the mouth | sangat marah; naik pitam | The coach was foaming at the mouth after the referee's terrible decision.
my lips are sealed | mulutku terkunci; rahasia aman | Don't worry about the surprise party, my lips are sealed.
lick one's lips | menanti-nanti dengan antusias | The fans were licking their lips at the thought of the final.
tongue-tied | kelu; tidak bisa berkata-kata | Anna was so nervous at the interview that she became completely tongue-tied.
put words into someone's mouth | menaruh kata-kata di mulut orang; memelintir ucapan | Don't put words into my mouth, I never said the plan was bad.
## Tangan
hand over fist | dengan sangat cepat (mendapatkan uang) | During the summer season, the beach cafés make money hand over fist.
sit on one's hands | tinggal diam; tidak bertindak apa-apa | The council sat on its hands while the old theatre slowly fell apart.
show one's hand | membuka kartu; menunjukkan niat asli | The buyer refused to show his hand until he had seen all the offers.
force someone's hand | memaksa seseorang mengambil tindakan | The leaked email forced the director's hand, and she resigned the next day.
know something like the back of one's hand | hafal di luar kepala; sangat mengenal | Ali grew up here, so he knows these streets like the back of his hand.
an old hand | orang berpengalaman; ahli | Ask Omar to fix it, he's an old hand at repairing bicycles.
a free hand | kebebasan bertindak; wewenang penuh | The new chef was given a free hand to change the whole menu.
hands down | dengan mudah; tanpa tandingan | This is hands down the best soup I have ever tasted.
hand someone something on a plate | memberikan sesuatu dengan mudah tanpa usaha | The defender's mistake handed the other team the victory on a plate.
live from hand to mouth | hidup pas-pasan; gali lubang tutup lubang | As young artists, they lived from hand to mouth in a tiny flat in Dublin.
## Kaki
put one's best foot forward | tampil maksimal; berusaha sebaik mungkin | On your first day at work, try to put your best foot forward.
have one foot in the grave | sudah uzur; satu kaki di liang kubur | My grandfather jokes that he has one foot in the grave, but he still swims every day.
have itchy feet | tidak bisa diam; berjiwa petualang | After two years in the same city, Yuki had itchy feet again.
never set foot in | tidak akan pernah menginjakkan kaki di | After the way they treated me, I'll never set foot in that shop again.
be rushed off one's feet | sangat sibuk; kewalahan berlarian | The nurses were rushed off their feet during the winter flu season.
on one's last legs | sekarat; hampir rusak total | My old car is on its last legs, I'll have to buy a new one soon.
have two left feet | kaku; tidak bisa berdansa sama sekali | I'd love to dance with you, but I'm afraid I have two left feet.
think on one's feet | berpikir cepat dalam situasi mendesak | Good teachers need to think on their feet when a lesson goes wrong.
not have a leg to stand on | tidak punya alasan/bukti pembelaan | Without a written contract, you don't have a leg to stand on.
sweep someone off their feet | membuat terpesona; memikat hati sepenuhnya | He swept her off her feet with his charm and his terrible jokes.
## Kepala
laugh one's head off | tertawa terbahak-bahak | The children laughed their heads off at the clown's silly tricks.
put heads together | berdiskusi bersama; memutar otak | If we put our heads together, I'm sure we'll find a solution.
heads will roll | ada yang akan dipecat; hukuman akan dijatuhkan | When the boss finds out about the lost files, heads will roll.
come to a head | mencapai puncaknya; genting | The dispute between the neighbours came to a head when one of them called the police.
need one's head examined | perlu diperiksa kewarasannya; tidak waras | You want to swim in the sea in January? You need your head examined!
bite someone's head off | membentak; memarahi secara tiba-tiba | I only asked if dinner was ready, there's no need to bite my head off!
can't make head nor tail of | tidak paham sama sekali | I can't make head nor tail of these instructions for the new washing machine.
go to one's head | membuat sombong; besar kepala karena pujian | Don't let all this praise go to your head, you still have a lot to learn.
head and shoulders above | jauh lebih unggul | As a singer, she is head and shoulders above the other contestants.
bang one's head against a brick wall | membenturkan kepala ke tembok; buang-buang tenaga | Trying to change the director's mind is like banging your head against a brick wall.
## Wajah dan hidung
keep one's nose clean | menjaga diri dari masalah | After his release, Diego kept his nose clean and found a steady job.
a slap in the face | tamparan bagi diri; sebuah penghinaan | Being passed over for the promotion was a real slap in the face for Anna.
keep a straight face | menahan tawa; menjaga ekspresi serius | When he walked in wearing that hat, I could hardly keep a straight face.
save face | menyelamatkan muka; menjaga martabat | To save face, the company called the failed product a test version.
face the music | menghadapi konsekuensi | You broke the window, Lukas, so now you'll have to face the music.
pull a long face | memasang muka muram; merengut | There's no point pulling a long face, we're not buying another video game.
fly in the face of | bertentangan dengan | His theory flies in the face of everything we know about the climate.
turn one's nose up at | memandang rendah; menolak dengan angkuh | My nephew turns his nose up at any vegetable that is green.
under someone's nose | tepat di depan mata | The keys were right under my nose the whole time I was searching.
poke one's nose into | ikut campur; usil | I wish our neighbour would stop poking her nose into our business.
## Bahu, punggung, leher, dan lengan
a chip on one's shoulder | menyimpan rasa iri/dendam; merasa minder | He has a chip on his shoulder because he never went to university.
behind someone's back | di belakang (tanpa sepengetahuan) | I can't believe they were laughing at me behind my back.
breathe down someone's neck | mengawasi dengan ketat; menempel terus | I can't concentrate with my manager breathing down my neck all day.
keep someone at arm's length | menjaga jarak dengan seseorang | Sofia is friendly, but she keeps new colleagues at arm's length.
turn one's back on | memunggungi; menolak membantu | He turned his back on a successful career in law to become a farmer.
rub shoulders with | bergaul dengan; akrab dengan | At the film festival, young directors rub shoulders with famous actors.
get someone's back up | membuat kesal; memantik kemarahan | His arrogant tone really gets my back up.
put one's back into it | bekerja dengan mengerahkan tenaga penuh | If you put your back into it, we'll finish painting the fence by lunchtime.
neck and neck | bersaing ketat; seimbang | The two runners were neck and neck until the very last metre.
twist someone's arm | membujuk dengan gigih; memaksa secara halus | I didn't want another dessert, but Omar twisted my arm.
## Jari, gigi, dan tulang
point the finger at | menuding; menyalahkan | When the project failed, everyone pointed the finger at the new manager.
have a finger in every pie | ikut campur dalam banyak hal | Our neighbour has a finger in every pie, from the school board to the local choir.
all fingers and thumbs | canggung; kaku; sakar | I'm all fingers and thumbs today, I've already dropped two cups.
not lift a finger | tidak mau membantu sedikit pun | My brother didn't lift a finger while we were cleaning the house.
wrap someone round one's little finger | mempermainkan; menaklukkan hati seseorang dengan mudah | Little Sofia has wrapped her grandfather round her little finger.
by the skin of one's teeth | nyaris sekali; tipis | We caught the last train by the skin of our teeth.
lie through one's teeth | berbohong tanpa rasa bersalah | He said he'd never seen the money, but he was lying through his teeth.
long in the tooth | sudah tua; lanjut usia | I'm getting a bit long in the tooth to sleep in a tent.
have a bone to pick with | ada urusan/ketidakpuasan yang harus diselesaikan | I have a bone to pick with you, Diego, why did you eat my sandwich?
make no bones about | blak-blakan; tidak ragu-ragu menyatakan | Camille made no bones about her dislike of the new design.
