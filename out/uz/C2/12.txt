# UNIT: Idiomalar III: tana aʼzolari | Quloq, koʻz, yurak, til, qoʻl, oyoq, bosh va boshqa tana aʼzolari bilan idiomalar
## Koʻz
give someone the evil eye | yomon koʻz bilan qarash, yomon koʻrish | The old man gave us the evil eye when our ball landed in his garden.
with one's eyes open | bilib turib, koʻra-bila | Ali took the risky job with his eyes open, knowing the pay was low at first.
pull the wool over someone's eyes | koʻz boyash, aldash | Don't try to pull the wool over my eyes, I know you didn't do your homework.
have an eye for | (biror narsani) yaxshi tushunish, fahmi boʻlish | Camille has an eye for colour, so ask her to choose the curtains.
make eyes at | koʻz suzish, qosh-koʻz qilish | The boy at the next table kept making eyes at Sofia all evening.
only have eyes for | (faqat) bir kishini koʻrish, oʻzgani pisand qilmaslik | There were many guests at the party, but Diego only had eyes for Ana.
not believe one's eyes | koʻziga ishonmaslik | When I saw the price of the tickets, I couldn't believe my eyes.
one's eyes are bigger than one's stomach | koʻzi toʻymaslik, yeb bitira olmaslik | Lukas filled three plates at the buffet, but his eyes were bigger than his stomach.
raise eyebrows | hayratga solish, eʼtiroz uygʻotish | His decision to wear trainers to the wedding raised a few eyebrows.
up to one's eyes | boshidan oshib yotish, band boʻlish | I'd love to help, but I'm up to my eyes in paperwork this week.
## Quloq
lend an ear | quloq solish, eshitish | If you ever need someone to talk to, I'm always happy to lend an ear.
fall on deaf ears | quloq solinmaslik, eʼtiborga olinmaslik | Our complaints about the noise fell on deaf ears for months.
keep one's ear to the ground | qulogʻi lola, xabardor boʻlib turish | Keep your ear to the ground and tell me if you hear about any job openings.
have someone's ear | gapini oʻtkaza olish, taʼsir qila olish | Omar has the director's ear, so ask him to mention your idea.
one's ears are burning | qulogʻi jaranglash, eslanayotgan boʻlish | Were your ears burning last night, Lukas? We were all talking about you.
grin from ear to ear | ogʻzi qulogʻiga yetish | When Emre saw his exam results, he was grinning from ear to ear.
wet behind the ears | ogʻzi sut ketmagan, tajribasiz | The new manager is a bit wet behind the ears, but he learns quickly.
music to one's ears | eshitishga yoqimli boʻlish, koʻngilga xush yoqish | When the boss said we could leave early, it was music to my ears.
go in one ear and out the other | bir qulogʻidan kirib, ikkinchisidan chiqib ketish | I tell my son to tidy his room, but it goes in one ear and out the other.
prick up one's ears | quloq solish, diqqat bilan eshitish | The dog pricked up its ears at the sound of the car in the drive.
## Yurak
one's heart goes out to | yuragi achishish, dardinga sherik boʻlish | Our hearts go out to all the families who lost their homes in the flood.
pour one's heart out | ichini toʻkish, sirini ochish | Late at night, Emre poured his heart out to his oldest friend.
with a heavy heart | ogʻir yurak bilan, gʻamgin | With a heavy heart, the family decided to sell the old farm.
heart and soul | bor vujudi bilan, jon-jahdi bilan | She put her heart and soul into building the village library.
close to one's heart | yurakka yaqin, aziz | Protecting the local forest is a cause very close to my heart.
not have the heart to | yuragi boʻlmaslik, raqsi kelmaslik | I didn't have the heart to tell him that his singing was terrible.
a heart of stone | tosh yurak, bagʻritosh | Only someone with a heart of stone could ignore a crying child.
one's heart skips a beat | yuragi orqaga tortish, hayajonlanish | My heart skipped a beat when I saw her name on the list of winners.
take heart | dalda topish, ruhlanish | Take heart from the fact that the second half of the course is much easier.
to one's heart's content | koʻnglidagicha, maroq bilan | At the seaside, the children could swim and play to their hearts' content.
## Til va ogʻiz
set tongues wagging | mish-mishlarga sabab boʻlish | Their late-night walks along the beach soon set tongues wagging in the village.
get one's tongue round | talaffuz qila olish, tilini keltirish | I still can't get my tongue round the name of that Welsh village.
button one's lip | tilini tiyish, ovoz chiqarmaslik | Button your lip, Omar, the surprise will be ruined if you say anything.
have a big mouth | sergʻayb, ogʻzida siri turmaydigan | Don't tell Lukas your secret, he has a big mouth.
keep one's mouth shut | ogʻzini yumish, sir saqlash | If anyone asks about the plan, just keep your mouth shut.
foam at the mouth | gʻazabdan oʻzini qoʻyishga joy topolmaslik | The coach was foaming at the mouth after the referee's terrible decision.
my lips are sealed | ogʻzimga qulf urilgan, hech kimga aytmayman | Don't worry about the surprise party, my lips are sealed.
lick one's lips | ogʻzining tanobi qochish, ishtiyoqi baland boʻlish | The fans were licking their lips at the thought of the final.
tongue-tied | tili aylanmay qolish, gapira olmay qolish | Anna was so nervous at the interview that she became completely tongue-tied.
put words into someone's mouth | aytmagan gapni toʻnkash, ogʻziga soʻz solish | Don't put words into my mouth, I never said the plan was bad.
## Qoʻl
hand over fist | tez surʼatda, moʻmaygina (pul topish) | During the summer season, the beach cafés make money hand over fist.
sit on one's hands | qoʻl qovushtirib oʻtirish, chora koʻrmaslik | The council sat on its hands while the old theatre slowly fell apart.
show one's hand | niyatini ochib qoʻyish, sirini bildirish | The buyer refused to show his hand until he had seen all the offers.
force someone's hand | majburlash, chorasiz qoldirish | The leaked email forced the director's hand, and she resigned the next day.
know something like the back of one's hand | besh qoʻday bilish, yaxshi bilish | Ali grew up here, so he knows these streets like the back of his hand.
an old hand | tajribali mutaxassis, pishiq mutaxassis | Ask Omar to fix it, he's an old hand at repairing bicycles.
a free hand | toʻliq erkinlik, ixtiyor | The new chef was given a free hand to change the whole menu.
hands down | shubhasiz, hech qanday shubhasiz | This is hands down the best soup I have ever tasted.
hand someone something on a plate | tayyor holda tutqazish, oltin tepsida taqdim etish | The defender's mistake handed the other team the victory on a plate.
live from hand to mouth | kunini zoʻrgʻa koʻrish, qoʻldan toʻqqizga uzatish | As young artists, they lived from hand to mouth in a tiny flat in Dublin.
## Oyoq
put one's best foot forward | bor kuchini koʻrsatish, harakat qilish | On your first day at work, try to put your best foot forward.
have one foot in the grave | bir oyogʻi gʻorda boʻlish, qarigan boʻlish | My grandfather jokes that he has one foot in the grave, but he still swims every day.
have itchy feet | sayohatga shay turish, tinch oʻtirolmaslik | After two years in the same city, Yuki had itchy feet again.
never set foot in | qadam bosmaslik, bormaslik | After the way they treated me, I'll never set foot in that shop again.
be rushed off one's feet | tinim bilmay yugurish, band boʻlish | The nurses were rushed off their feet during the winter flu season.
on one's last legs | poyoni yetib qolgan, ishdan chiqqan | My old car is on its last legs, I'll have to buy a new one soon.
have two left feet | raqs tushishni bilmaslik, qoʻpol boʻlish | I'd love to dance with you, but I'm afraid I have two left feet.
think on one's feet | tez qaror qabul qilish, vaziyatga moslashish | Good teachers need to think on their feet when a lesson goes wrong.
not have a leg to stand on | haqiqatga asosi boʻlmaslik, himoya qilolmaslik | Without a written contract, you don't have a leg to stand on.
sweep someone off their feet | lol qoldirish, maftun etish | He swept her off her feet with his charm and his terrible jokes.
## Bosh
laugh one's head off | qah-qah urib kulish | The children laughed their heads off at the clown's silly tricks.
put heads together | bosh qotirish, maslahatlashish | If we put our heads together, I'm sure we'll find a solution.
heads will roll | jazo muqarrar boʻlish, bosh ketish | When the boss finds out about the lost files, heads will roll.
come to a head | choʻqqisiga chiqish, keskinlashish | The dispute between the neighbours came to a head when one of them called the police.
need one's head examined | axta boʻlish, aqldan ozgan boʻlish | You want to swim in the sea in January? You need your head examined!
bite someone's head off | jerkib berish, jahlini chiqarish | I only asked if dinner was ready, there's no need to bite my head off!
can't make head nor tail of | tagiga yetolmaslik, tushunolmaslik | I can't make head nor tail of these instructions for the new washing machine.
go to one's head | bosqinchilik qilish, manmanlikka berilish | Don't let all this praise go to your head, you still have a lot to learn.
head and shoulders above | boshqalardan ustun turish, oʻziga toʻq boʻlish | As a singer, she is head and shoulders above the other contestants.
bang one's head against a brick wall | devorga gapirish, befoyda harakat qilish | Trying to change the director's mind is like banging your head against a brick wall.
## Yuz va burun
keep one's nose clean | boshni balodan uzoq tutish | After his release, Diego kept his nose clean and found a steady job.
a slap in the face | yuzga urilgan tarsaki, haqorat | Being passed over for the promotion was a real slap in the face for Anna.
keep a straight face | jiddiy qiyofani saqlash, kulmaslik | When he walked in wearing that hat, I could hardly keep a straight face.
save face | obroʻni saqlab qolish, yuzini saqlab qolish | To save face, the company called the failed product a test version.
face the music | oqibatiga chidash, javob berish | You broke the window, Lukas, so now you'll have to face the music.
pull a long face | qovogʻini solish, xafa koʻrinish | There's no point pulling a long face, we're not buying another video game.
fly in the face of | zid kelish, inkor etish | His theory flies in the face of everything we know about the climate.
turn one's nose up at | burun jiqshiltish, mensimaslik | My nephew turns his nose up at any vegetable that is green.
under someone's nose | burun ostida, koʻz oʻngida | The keys were right under my nose the whole time I was searching.
poke one's nose into | burnini suqish, aralashish | I wish our neighbour would stop poking her nose into our business.
## Omuz va qoʻl
a chip on one's shoulder | oʻkinch, alamzada boʻlish | He has a chip on his shoulder because he never went to university.
behind someone's back | orqadan ish koʻrish, sirtdan | I can't believe they were laughing at me behind my back.
breathe down someone's neck | ensasini qotirish, taʼqib qilish | I can't concentrate with my manager breathing down my neck all day.
keep someone at arm's length | masofa saqlash, yaqinlashtirmaslik | Sofia is friendly, but she keeps new colleagues at arm's length.
turn one's back on | yuz oʻgirish, voz kechish | He turned his back on a successful career in law to become a farmer.
rub shoulders with | elka turish, muloqot qilish | At the film festival, young directors rub shoulders with famous actors.
get someone's back up | gʻashiga tegish, jahlini chiqarish | His arrogant tone really gets my back up.
put one's back into it | bor kuchini berish, qattiq kirishish | If you put your back into it, we'll finish painting the fence by lunchtime.
neck and neck | yonma-yon, tengma-teng | The two runners were neck and neck until the very last metre.
twist someone's arm | koʻndirish, majburlash | I didn't want another dessert, but Omar twisted my arm.
## Barmoq, tish va suyak
point the finger at | ayblash, aybni qoʻyish | When the project failed, everyone pointed the finger at the new manager.
have a finger in every pie | hamma ishga aralashish | Our neighbour has a finger in every pie, from the school board to the local choir.
all fingers and thumbs | qoʻli ishga bormaslik, shoshaloq | I'm all fingers and thumbs today, I've already dropped two cups.
not lift a finger | barmogʻini ham qimirlatmaslik | My brother didn't lift a finger while we were cleaning the house.
wrap someone round one's little finger | istagandek boshqarish, oʻynatish | Little Sofia has wrapped her grandfather round her little finger.
by the skin of one's teeth | zoʻrgʻa, qil ustida | We caught the last train by the skin of our teeth.
lie through one's teeth | uyalmay yolgʻon gapirish | He said he'd never seen the money, but he was lying through his teeth.
long in the tooth | yoshi bir joyga borib qolgan | I'm getting a bit long in the tooth to sleep in a tent.
have a bone to pick with | dardi bor boʻlish, shikoyati boʻlish | I have a bone to pick with you, Diego, why did you eat my sandwich?
make no bones about | ochiqchasiga aytish, yashirmaslik | Camille made no bones about her dislike of the new design.
