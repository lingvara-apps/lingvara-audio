# UNIT: Idiomlar I | Ish, muvaffaqiyat, xarakter, munosabatlar
## Mehnat va harakat
keep one's nose to the grindstone | tinmay mehnat qilish | If you keep your nose to the grindstone this term, you will pass the exam easily.
work one's fingers to the bone | jon-jahdi bilan ishlash | Her parents worked their fingers to the bone so that she could study abroad.
pull out all the stops | bor kuchini ishga solish | The hotel pulled out all the stops for the wedding on Saturday.
roll up one's sleeves | qiyin ishga kirishish | It's time to roll up our sleeves and finish the report before Friday.
put one's shoulder to the wheel | ishga qattiq kirishish | If everyone puts their shoulder to the wheel, we can open the café next month.
elbow grease | jismoniy kuch, zahmat | A little elbow grease will make that old table look brand new.
work like a dog | qora ter toʻkib ishlash | Diego worked like a dog all summer to pay off his student loan.
leave no stone unturned | barcha imkoniyatni ishga solish | The detectives promised to leave no stone unturned in their search for the missing girl.
a labour of love | safarbarlik zavqi, koʻngildan qilingan ish | Restoring the old sailing boat was a labour of love for my grandfather.
burn the candle at both ends | oʻzini oʻtga-choʻgʻga urib charchatish | Working nights and studying by day, Ali was burning the candle at both ends.
## Ish joyida
climb the corporate ladder | lavozim zinapoyasidan yuqoriga koʻtarilish | She spent fifteen years climbing the corporate ladder before starting her own firm.
a dead-end job | istiqbolsiz ish | He felt trapped in a dead-end job with no chance of promotion.
the rat race | mashaqqatli hayot poygasi | Tired of the rat race, Sofia moved to a small village on the coast.
pass the buck | masʼuliyatni boshqalarga yuklash | Every department passed the buck, so nobody fixed the problem for months.
wear many hats | bir nechta vazifani bir vaqtning oʻzida bajarish | In a small start-up you have to wear many hats, from sales to accounting.
get the sack | ishdan haydalish | He got the sack for arriving late to work almost every day.
step into someone's shoes | birovning oʻrnini egallash | Nobody expected Yuki to step into the director's shoes so quickly.
have a lot on one's plate | boshi qashishib ketish | I'd love to help, but I have a lot on my plate this week.
the powers that be | rahbarlar, yuqori idoralar | The powers that be have decided to close the branch in December.
run a tight ship | tartib-intizom bilan boshqarish | The new manager runs a tight ship, and nobody dares to miss a deadline.
## Muvaffaqiyat va gʻalaba
hit the jackpot | omadi chopish, katta yutuqqa erishish | The small bakery hit the jackpot when a famous food blogger praised its pies.
come up trumps | kutilmaganda muvaffaqiyat qozonish | Our youngest player came up trumps and scored the winning goal.
make the grade | talab darajasiga chiqish | Only three of the twenty applicants made the grade for the final interview.
pass with flying colours | aʼlo bahoga oʻtish | Omar passed his driving test with flying colours on the first attempt.
take the world by storm | butun dunyoni lol qoldirish | Their first album took the world by storm and sold millions of copies.
the sky's the limit | imkoniyatlar cheksiz | With your talent and energy, the sky's the limit.
rest on one's laurels | erishilgan yutuq bilan kifoyalanib qolish | The company won an award last year, but it can't afford to rest on its laurels.
go from strength to strength | tobora kuchayib borish | Since the new coach arrived, the team has gone from strength to strength.
a feather in one's cap | faxr tuygʻusi, yutuq | Winning the international contract was a real feather in her cap.
make a name for oneself | nom qozonish, tanilish | Lukas made a name for himself as a designer of eco-friendly houses.
## Muvaffaqiyatsizlik va qayta boshlash
fall flat | muvaffaqiyatsizlikka uchrash, oʻxshamay qolish | His joke fell flat, and the room went completely silent.
go down the drain | suvga sovurilish, yoʻq boʻlish | Months of planning went down the drain when the sponsor pulled out.
bite the dust | barbod boʻlish, yoʻq boʻlib ketish | Another small bookshop in our street has bitten the dust this year.
miss the boat | fursatni qoʻldan boy berish | Prices have doubled, so I'm afraid we've missed the boat on that flat.
a flash in the pan | vaqtinchalik muvaffaqiyat | Critics said his early success was just a flash in the pan.
go belly up | kasallikka uchrash (bankrot boʻlish) | The airline went belly up after only two years in business.
come a cropper | qattiq xatoga yoʻl qoʻyish, yiqilish | He came a cropper when he tried to expand the business too fast.
pick up the pieces | tarqoq hayotni yigʻishtirish | After the fire, the family slowly began to pick up the pieces.
get back on one's feet | oyoqqa turib olish | It took Maria a year to get back on her feet after the divorce.
learn the hard way | achchiq tajribadan saboq chiqarish | I learned the hard way that you should always back up your files.
## Tavakkal va qaror qabul qilish
take the plunge | qatʼiy qadam qoʻyish | After years of renting, they finally took the plunge and bought a house.
throw caution to the wind | ehtiyotkorlikni unutib tavakkal qilish | For once, Camille threw caution to the wind and booked a trip around the world.
play with fire | oʻt bilan oʻynashish | Ignoring the safety rules on a building site is playing with fire.
bite the bullet | tishni tishga qoʻyib chidash | I hate going to the dentist, but I'll have to bite the bullet.
burn one's bridges | orqadagi koʻpriklarni kuydirib yuborish | Don't burn your bridges with your old boss; you may need a reference.
a leap in the dark | qorongʻilikka tashlangan qadam | Moving to a country where she didn't speak the language was a leap in the dark.
put all one's eggs in one basket | barcha imkoniyatni bir joyga tikish | Investors are advised not to put all their eggs in one basket.
hedge one's bets | tavakkalni kamaytirish | Emre is hedging his bets by applying to universities in three countries.
stick one's neck out | xavf-xatarga bosh tikish | She stuck her neck out to defend her colleague in front of the board.
the die is cast | qaror qabul qilindi, ortga yoʻl yoʻq | We've signed the contract, so the die is cast.
## Ijobiy xarakter
salt of the earth | olijanob inson | The farmers in this valley are the salt of the earth.
a rough diamond | qoʻpol koʻringan yaxshi inson | He seems rude at first, but he's a rough diamond with a kind heart.
a straight shooter | ochiqkoʻngil va rostgoʻy kishi | You can trust Anna's opinion because she's a straight shooter.
a class act | olijanob va namunali inson | Even after losing the final, the champion behaved like a real class act.
a safe pair of hands | ishonchli inson | The board chose him as treasurer because he is a safe pair of hands.
a tower of strength | ishonchli tayanch | During my illness, my sister was a tower of strength for the whole family.
have a good head on one's shoulders | aqli raso boʻlish | Don't worry about your son; he has a good head on his shoulders.
one in a million | tengi yoʻq, noyob | A nurse like Maria is one in a million; she remembers every patient's name.
tough as nails | temirdek baquvvat, qatʼiy | My grandmother was tough as nails and raised six children alone.
a pillar of the community | hurmatga sazovor inson | The old doctor was a pillar of the community for forty years.
## Salbiy xarakter

a sore loser | magʻlubiyatni tan olmaydigan kishi | Don't be a sore loser; shake hands and congratulate the winner.
a nosy parker | burnini har joyga suqadigan kishi | Some nosy parker told the whole street about our argument.
a slippery customer | ishonchsiz, sirgʻanchiq kishi | The salesman was a slippery customer who never gave a straight answer.
a smart alec | oʻzini aqlli sanaydigan kishi | Stop being a smart alec and answer the question properly.
a stick-in-the-mud | eski qarashlarga yopishib olgan kishi | Don't be such a stick-in-the-mud; try the new restaurant with us.
a backseat driver | keraksiz maslahat beruvchi | My brother is a terrible backseat driver who criticises every turn I take.
a lone wolf | yolgʻiz yurishni yoqtiruvchi | As a journalist, he was a lone wolf who preferred to work alone.
a loose cannon | oldindan aytib boʻlmaydigan kishi | The coach considers the outspoken striker a loose cannon.
a nasty piece of work | yomon niyatli kishi | Be careful with the new landlord; he's a nasty piece of work.

thick as thieves | suv yuqtirmas doʻstlar | Those two have been thick as thieves since primary school.

an old flame | eski muhabbat | At the reunion, Omar bumped into an old flame from university.
